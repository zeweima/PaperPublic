# [Microbial Community Traits and Necromass Dynamics Shape Soil Carbon Accumulation](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70906)

**Authors:** Wankun Pan, Sheng Tang, Wolfgang Wanek et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70906
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Organic fertilization drives SOC gains via both POC and MAOC pools (+77% after 80 years), while inorganic fertilization triggers microbial "mining" that restricts accumulation to MAOC only.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Mechanisms linking fertilization type, microbial community strategy, and long-term soil organic carbon (SOC) accumulation across particulate (POC) and mineral-associated (MAOC) pools are poorly resolved.
- **Method:** Combined mesocosm/field experiments with a global meta-analysis of field experiments spanning 0–120 years across diverse soil types; compared organic vs. inorganic fertilization treatments.
- **Key result:** Organic fertilization increased total SOC 77% after 80 years (3× greater than inorganic inputs) by building both POC and MAOC; inorganic fertilization induced an oligotrophic "mining" strategy — upregulated degradation of complex organic matter under C-limited conditions — restricting gains to MAOC alone.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that sustained SOC sequestration requires aligning nutrient inputs with microbial community traits and necromass dynamics, not just carbon input quantity; supports organic amendment strategies for long-term C storage.
- **Caveats:** Abstract is truncated; full magnitude of microbial trait differences and soil-type variability in meta-analysis unclear from available text. Mechanisms inferred partly from meta-analysis correlations.
