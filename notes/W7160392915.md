# [Physical Mechanisms Governing the Response of Oceanic pCO₂ in the Northern South China Sea to Tropical Cyclones](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl118879)

**Authors:** Qiong Wu, Keyu Hu, Zhenxin Ruan et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-02 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl118879
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Tropical cyclone frequency suppresses surface pCO₂ in the N. South China Sea via SST cooling in the NW and freshwater-driven dilution plus suppressed DIC upwelling in the NE.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Tropical cyclones (TCs) perturb ocean–atmosphere CO₂ exchange, but the regional mechanisms driving pCO₂ responses in the Northern South China Sea (NSCS) across interannual timescales are unresolved.
- **Method:** Stepwise regression analysis linking TC genesis frequency (TCF) to interannual pCO₂ variability in two sub-regions of the NSCS; drivers decomposed into SST, sea surface salinity (SSS), and dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC).
- **Key result:** In the NW SCS, TC-induced SST reduction dominates pCO₂ suppression (SST explains 35.83% of variance). In the NE SCS, SSS is the leading factor (30.94%), followed by SST (24.41%) and DIC (16.14%); freshwater input from TCs dilutes surface CO₂ and inhibits DIC-rich subsurface upwelling via suppressed vertical mixing.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes region-specific physical pathways by which TC activity modulates oceanic carbon uptake; relevant for projecting South China Sea carbon budgets under changing TC climatology.
- **Caveats:** Analysis is interannual and correlational; biological feedbacks (e.g., phytoplankton response to TC mixing) not resolved; limited to NSCS domain.
