# [Understanding the Influence of Soil Moisture on Machine Learning Predictions of Photosynthesis and Evapotranspiration](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121483)

**Authors:** Daniel Power, Miguel Angel Rico-Ramirez, Pierre Gentine et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-04 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121483
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

ET and GPP respond to distinct soil moisture mechanisms: spatial-scale matching drives ET, temporal memory (deeper SM) drives GPP, improving ML predictions up to 30% at driest sites.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Soil moisture (SM) data exist at many spatiotemporal scales; it is unclear which SM representation best informs ML-based flux predictions of ET and gross primary productivity (GPP) across aridity gradients.
- **Method:** ML models trained on eddy covariance flux data across semi-arid to arid sites; compared multiple SM inputs — in situ near-surface SM, SM memory (lag features indicative of deeper root-zone control) — as predictors of ET and GPP.
- **Key result:** In situ near-surface SM improves ET predictions at semi-arid/arid sites; SM memory improves GPP predictions by up to 30% at the driest sites. ET and GPP are governed by distinct SM mechanisms.
- **Why it matters:** Process-guided feature engineering — matching SM representation to the controlling mechanism (spatial scale for ET, temporal depth for GPP) — can substitute for root-zone SM observations that are rarely available in ungauged regions, enabling better flux upscaling.
- **Caveats:** Results concentrate on semi-arid to arid sites; generalizability to humid or energy-limited ecosystems unclear from abstract. SM memory operationalization and lag window choices not detailed.
