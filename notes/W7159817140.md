# [Varying the Combination of Hydrological Models in Time and Space: Toward a More Accurate Representation of Streamflow in Large‐Sample Hydrology](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042272)

**Authors:** C. Thébault, W. J. M. Knoben, N. Addor et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042272
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Dynamic, time-varying ensemble weighting across 78 FUSE models and 559 US catchments reduces high-flow/low-flow trade-offs and sampling uncertainty versus static approaches.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Single-model-structure hydrology fixes parameters uniformly in space and time, poorly representing regime shifts (e.g., seasonal low-flow vs. high-flow behavior) and hiding structural uncertainty.
- **Method:** FUSE framework generated 78 model structures applied to 559 CONUS catchments; each model calibrated separately for high-flow and low-flow performance (156 simulations/catchment). A model-agnostic dynamic combiner identifies past conditions analogous to the current time step, ranks models by performance under those analogs, and weights the *m* best models equally at each time step.
- **Key result:** Dynamic combination mitigates competing-objective trade-offs (high vs. low flows) relative to static weighting and reduces sampling uncertainty across the ensemble; specific KGE or NSE improvements not reported in abstract.
- **Why it matters:** Provides an interpretable, computationally cheap alternative to neural-network streamflow emulators; applicable to forecasting and potentially ungauged catchments; makes structural model uncertainty explicit rather than absorbed into a single calibrated parameter set.
- **Caveats:** Cannot predict outside the ensemble prediction envelope (no extrapolation); current implementation is retrospective—extension to true forecasting and ungauged basins requires further work; method relies on analog-based similarity, which may underperform in non-stationary climates with no historical analogs.
