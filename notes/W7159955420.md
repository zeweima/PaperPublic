# [Single-Cell/Molecule Energetic Elucidation of Shuttle-Enhanced Extracellular Electron Transfer: Implications for Iron and Phosphorus Mobilization](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.5c17543)

**Authors:** Yuke Fan, Jialin Chi, Liping Fang et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-02 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.5c17543
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Electron shuttle (ES) binding energetics at cell-mineral interfaces, quantified for the first time at single-cell/molecule scale, facilitate extracellular electron transfer and drive Fe and P mobilization.

## Key result

Thermodynamic measurements of microbial cell-mineral and ES-cell binding energetics show ES compounds lower interfacial energy barriers, enhancing extracellular electron transfer (EET) and significantly increasing bioavailability of iron and phosphorus.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Mechanisms linking shuttle-mediated extracellular electron transfer (EET) to iron and phosphorus mobilization lacked quantitative thermodynamic grounding at the cell and molecular scale.
- **Method:** Single-cell/single-molecule force spectroscopy and related techniques to quantify binding energetics between microbial cells, electron shuttles (ES), and mineral surfaces; bridging molecular to cellular interfacial thermodynamics.
- **Key result:** ES-cell and cell-mineral binding energetics thermodynamically favor EET; this enhancement significantly increases Fe and P bioavailability. First quantification of microbial cell-mineral and ES-cell binding energetics reported.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes mechanistic basis for ES-mediated EET in nutrient cycling; opens control strategies for nutrient mobilization, pollutant transformation, and sustainable bioenergy applications.
- **Caveats:** Abstract is truncated — full experimental scope, specific energy values, and breadth of mineral/shuttle types tested are unclear from available text.
