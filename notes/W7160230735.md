# [Genomic ancestry predicts rapid responses to drought across spatiotemporal scales](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2531142123)

**Authors:** Rozenn M. Pineau, Natalia Bercovich, Loren H. Rieseberg et al.
**Venue:** PNAS · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2531142123
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Admixture from divergent ancestral lineages underlies polygenic drought adaptation in a agricultural weed, with fluctuating climate selection preserving drought alleles over a century.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How genome-wide diversity responds to drought across within-generation to century-long timescales, and whether admixture between ancestral lineages aids adaptive responses to contemporary climate and land-use change.
- **Method:** Combined drought survival experiment on *Amaranthus tuberculatus* accessions from paired agricultural and natural populations + herbarium genomic time series spanning ~100 years; admixture mapping to identify drought-associated loci; allele-frequency tracking against historical climate records.
- **Key result:** Admixture mapping identified 43 independent drought-protective loci across nearly all chromosomes; allele frequencies shift rapidly — drought alleles increase in hot/dry years and are selected against in cool/wet years; this fluctuating selection signal is stronger over century-long than shorter intervals, indicating adaptive lag.
- **Why it matters:** Fluctuating selection maintains polygenic drought variation rather than fixing it, providing a reservoir for ongoing adaptation; ancestry-informed genomics can predict climate-adaptive capacity in weeds and potentially other species.
- **Caveats:** Findings are from a single species with an unusual admixed history; generalizability to non-weedy or non-admixed species is unclear; herbarium time series may have geographic sampling biases.
