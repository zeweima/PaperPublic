# [Ecosystem‐Scale Methane Emissions From Peatlands of the Hudson Bay Lowlands](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009439)

**Authors:** A. Bieniada, E.R. Humphreys
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009439
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Permafrost-free fens emit ~60% more CH₄ than co-located bogs; soil temperature, moisture, and GPP jointly predict flux better than water table or air temperature alone.

## Summary

- **Problem:** CH₄ emissions from the Hudson Bay Lowlands (HBL), the largest contiguous peatland complex in North America, are poorly constrained in magnitude and environmental controls across peatland types including permafrost-affected sites.
- **Method:** Eddy covariance measurements across 16 site-years (April–November) at four HBL peatlands: a permafrost-free treed fen (7 yr), co-located bog (6 yr), permafrost peat plateau near Hudson Bay coast (1 yr), and co-located thawed peatland (2 yr). Predictors tested include air temperature, water table depth, soil temperature, soil moisture, and GPP.
- **Key result:** Average seasonal CH₄ emissions ranged from 2.6 g CH₄ m⁻² (permafrost peat plateau; driest/coolest) to 6.0 g CH₄ m⁻² (permafrost-free fen; wettest/warmest). The bog was ~40% lower than the adjacent fen (3.8 vs. 6.0 g CH₄ m⁻²); the thawed peatland was ~60% higher than the intact permafrost plateau (4.1 vs. 2.6 g CH₄ m⁻²). Combined spatiotemporal variability was best explained by soil temperature, soil moisture, and GPP (positively correlated); daily flux showed hysteresis with soil temperature, and water table depth was the primary day-to-day predictor.
- **Why it matters:** Warming will likely increase HBL CH₄ emissions overall, but permafrost-affected peatlands show divergent trajectories: drying may moderate emissions from intact plateaus, while thaw and wetting could amplify them. Improved predictor selection (soil vs. air temperature; GPP inclusion) informs upscaling and model benchmarking for this critical peatland region.
- **Caveats:** Short record at the permafrost and thawed sites (1–2 site-years); seasonal window (April–November) excludes any winter flux contribution; single geographic cluster limits transferability across HBL heterogeneity.
