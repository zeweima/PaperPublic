# [Probabilistic urban flood prediction with multivariate assimilation of sewer and surface observations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2026.126065)

**Authors:** Bomi Kim, Yaewon Lee, Seungsoo Lee et al.
**Venue:** Water Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2026.126065
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Particle-filter data assimilation coupling sewer-level and surface-depth observations into a 1D–2D hydrodynamic model raises urban flood-extent skill (CSI) 34% over the open-loop baseline.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Urban flood prediction must contend with aging drainage infrastructure, rainfall uncertainty, and poorly understood sewer–surface interactions; few DA frameworks jointly handle all three or test observation-network design.
- **Method:** Particle-filter DA coupled to a 1D–2D hydrodynamic model; synthetic experiments on an urban catchment testbed in Osaka, Japan; tested univariate (sewer-only or surface-only) and multivariate (both) assimilation across varying sensor count, placement, and update frequency.
- **Key result:** Multivariate DA improved CSI from 0.64 to 0.86 (+34.4%); sewer-level-only assimilation outperformed surface-depth-only in accuracy and consistency; frequent updates and denser sensor networks reduced errors; downstream sewer sensors provided more stable constraints than upstream ones under sparse updating.
- **Why it matters:** Provides quantitative guidance on sensor placement and update strategies for operational urban flood forecasting; demonstrates particle filters can capture nonlinear sewer–surface coupling, supporting infrastructure resilience planning in data-scarce settings.
- **Caveats:** Evaluated on synthetic (not real-event) experiments; single catchment testbed limits transferability; real-time computational cost of particle filter at operational scale not assessed.
