# [Global Vegetation Greening Is Exacerbating Soil Dryness](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70901)

**Authors:** Z. Qu, Li X, Josep Peñuelas et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70901
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Vegetation greening amplifies soil dryness across 59–71% of vegetated land, driven by rising transpiration dominance, and most Earth system models misrepresent this feedback.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Global greening and soil drying are co-occurring, but the causal contribution of vegetation greenness to soil drought intensification and declining soil moisture trends was unquantified.
- **Method:** Evaluated vegetation effect on soil drought (ESD) and soil moisture trend (EST) from 1982 to 2020 across the global aridity gradient using observational data; compared against precipitation and temperature effects and assessed Earth system model performance.
- **Key result:** Negative ESD (exacerbated drought) in 71% of vegetated areas; negative EST (accelerated drying trend) in 59%. Vegetation effects exceeded those of both precipitation and temperature in 19% (ESD) and 44% (EST) of vegetated places. Effects peaked in semi-arid/dry sub-humid zones and intensified over time, driven by increasing transpiration share of evapotranspiration. Many Earth system models poorly reproduce and even distort temporal evolution of these effects.
- **Why it matters:** Vegetation-driven soil drying is a largely unaccounted positive feedback in the hydrological cycle; underestimation in models means water resource projections under greening scenarios are likely optimistic, with direct implications for drought risk adaptation.
- **Caveats:** Effect attribution method and specific datasets not detailed in abstract; model evaluation scope unclear; causality inferred from statistical relationships over a single 39-year window.
