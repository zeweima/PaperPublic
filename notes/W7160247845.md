# [Coordinated stomatal, mesophyll, and biochemical functions in photosynthetic responses to heat and dryness](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2605032123)

**Authors:** Xingyu Hu, Suan Chin Wong, Graham D. Farquhar
**Venue:** PNAS · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2605032123
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Stomatal and mesophyll conductances coordinate to buffer CO₂ diffusion under heat and elevated vapor pressure deficit, keeping biochemical colimitation near current atmospheric CO₂ (~400 μmol mol⁻¹).

## Summary

- **Problem:** Temperature and leaf-to-air vapor pressure difference (Δe) are intrinsically linked, making it difficult to isolate their individual effects on photosynthesis; mesophyll conductance (gm) responses to heat and high evaporative demand are poorly understood, as are the conditions for biochemical colimitation of Rubisco carboxylation and RuBP regeneration.
- **Method:** Controlled gas exchange and chlorophyll fluorescence measurements on cotton, sunflower, and dwarf bean; Δe held at 1 and 2 kPa while leaf temperature (T_leaf) varied 20–40 °C across five CO₂ levels (150–800 μmol mol⁻¹), decoupling temperature from Δe effects.
- **Key result:** gm partly counteracts increases in stomatal conductance to CO₂ (gsc) at high temperatures and declines in gsc at elevated Δe; coordination stabilizes chloroplast-to-ambient CO₂ ratio (Cc/Ca) across tested T_leaf and Δe ranges. Cc/Ca is more conservative with increasing T_leaf at Ca ≤ 400 μmol mol⁻¹ than at elevated Ca. The Rubisco-to-RuBP regeneration limitation transition remains near Ca = 400 μmol mol⁻¹ across all tested conditions.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that plants actively maintain biochemical colimitation near current atmospheric CO₂, implying photosynthetic models and land-surface schemes should account for coordinated gsc–gm responses rather than treating them independently under heat and drought stress.
- **Caveats:** Three C3 crop species only; results may not generalize to C4 plants, woody species, or field conditions where additional stresses (nutrient, water deficit) co-occur; Δe range (1–2 kPa) may not capture extreme drought scenarios.
