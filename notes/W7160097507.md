# [Energetic constraints on tropical precipitation changes under stratospheric aerosol geoengineering: a topical review](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6714)

**Authors:** Anu Xavier, Govindasamy Bala, Thejas Kallihosur
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6714

**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Two planetary energy budget constraints — global fast/slow adjustment and interhemispheric asymmetry — together explain most large-scale tropical precipitation changes under stratospheric aerosol geoengineering.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Stratospheric aerosol geoengineering (SAG) alters tropical precipitation in ways that are mechanistically fragmented across the literature; no unified framework exists to diagnose or design SAG strategies that avoid harmful hydrological side effects.
- **Method:** Topical review synthesizing SAG literature around two energetic constraints: (a) global energy budget via fast adjustment / slow response decomposition, and (b) interhemispheric energy imbalance controlling ITCZ position; also addresses stratospheric heating effects from sulfate aerosols on the precipitation budget.
- **Key result:** Combined application of the two energetic constraints explains most large-scale tropical precipitation changes simulated under SAG; sulfate-specific stratospheric heating modifies the fast-adjustment component in ways distinct from other SRM approaches.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a simple, physics-based framework for evaluating and designing SAG deployment strategies that minimize tropical precipitation disruption; identifies model simulation suites and research gaps needed to operationalize the framework.
- **Caveats:** Review scope limited to SAG (sulfate aerosols); applicability to other SRM methods (marine cloud brightening, space mirrors) requires further testing; regional precipitation responses within the tropics may not be fully captured by large-scale energetic constraints alone.
