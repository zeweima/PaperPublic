# [Tree community resource economics control soil food web multifunctionality](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10455-1)

**Authors:** Ludovic Henneron, David A. Wardle, Matty P. Berg et al.
**Venue:** Nature · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10455-1
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Dominant tree species' resource economics — not species richness — drive soil food web multifunctionality across European forests via litter quality and microclimate.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How plant community properties regulate ecosystem functioning through soil food web energy fluxes is poorly understood, particularly whether tree functional strategies or diversity are the dominant controls.
- **Method:** Food web energetics approach applied across European forests spanning gradients in tree species composition (resource-acquisitive vs. resource-conservative dominants) and richness (monocultures vs. three-species mixtures); soil trophic functions quantified at multiple levels.
- **Key result:** Resource-acquisitive tree communities promoted faster rates of multiple soil trophic functions than resource-conservative communities; tree species composition explained a portion of variation in soil food web multifunctionality comparable to biogeographic location effects. Three-species mixtures showed weakly negative effects on soil food web multifunctionality relative to monocultures despite aboveground overyielding in tree biomass.
- **Why it matters:** Climate-driven shifts in tree community composition (e.g., toward more conservative species at higher latitudes/altitudes) may reduce forest soil functioning; diversity effects above- and belowground can diverge, complicating forest management for multifunctionality.
- **Caveats:** Observational design limits causal inference; only three-species mixtures tested — higher diversity effects unclear; European forests may not generalize to tropical or boreal systems.
