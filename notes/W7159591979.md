# [Multi-Sensor Spatiotemporal Fusion for 30-m Daily Gapless Snow Cover Mapping](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025WR041155)

**Authors:** Jinhang Wu, Xueliang Zhang, Pengfeng Xiao et al.

**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10

## TL;DR

An adaptive ESTARFM-based framework fusing MODIS, VIIRS, Landsat, and Sentinel-2 generates 30-m daily gapless snow cover that cuts snow phenology error roughly in half vs. 500-m products.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Existing spatiotemporal fusion approaches for snow cover are bottlenecked by cloud contamination and sparse fine-resolution observations, preventing gapless, large-scale 30-m daily mapping in heterogeneous mountainous terrain.
- **Method:** Adaptive time-series ESTARFM framework applied over Urumqi, Xinjiang (14,193 km², Tianshan Mountains), hydrological year Sep 2022–Aug 2023. Coarse time series: Terra/Aqua MODIS + S-NPP VIIRS (500-m, daily); fine time series: Landsat-8/9 OLI + Sentinel-2A/B MSI (30-m). OLS harmonization aligns sensors (R² > 0.86 for all band pairs). Per-pixel adaptive selection of nearest valid coarse–fine image pairs eliminates the requirement for gapless fine-resolution input. Spatiotemporal interpolation (9-day window + inverse-distance weighting) gap-fills remaining coarse gaps (28.8% of pixels). SNOMAP applied to fused NDSI + green + NIR to produce binary snow maps. Validated against 10 CMA stations (3,525 records) and Copernicus GFSC (Alps cross-test).
- **Key result:** Fused NDSI R = 0.776–0.964 across snow seasons; highest accuracy in stable period (R = 0.926–0.964, MAE = 0.102–0.150, RMSE = 0.151–0.199). Ground-truth binary snow OA = 0.88, Kappa = 0.75 for 30-m vs. OA = 0.81, Kappa = 0.62 for 500-m. Snow phenology errors: 30-m averages 9 days (SCD), 5 days (SCOD), 6 days (SCED) vs. ~17, 11, 13 days for 500-m. Below 3,500 m elevation and on slopes ≤25°, 30-m detects more snow than 500-m; above 3,500 m, 500-m overestimates due to mixed-pixel effects. Alps comparison: omission error 0.13 (fused 30-m) vs. 0.11 (GFSC which uses SAR); fused data achieves 100% spatial coverage vs. GFSC mean 50.4%.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a practical, open-data pipeline (all inputs via GEE) for generating long-term 30-m daily gapless snow cover in data-sparse, cloud-prone mountain regions critical for snowmelt-fed water resources; halves phenology estimation error relative to standard MODIS products and resolves fine-scale terrain effects invisible at 500-m.
- **Caveats:** Single-year study period coincided with a drought year (SPEI = −1.89), limiting interannual representativeness. Per-pixel adaptive fusion can introduce mosaic artifacts during rapid accumulation/melt. Optical-only approach underperforms SAR-augmented products (GFSC) in cloudy or shadowed terrain. Coarse gap-filling artifacts degrade fusion when >9 consecutive cloudy days occur. Cross-sensor harmonization via OLS does not fully account for differing view/solar geometries or signal-to-noise ratios.
