# [Different Tree Recruitment Patterns at Treelines Are Associated With the Atlantic Multi‐Decadal Oscillation Across the Northern Hemisphere](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70893)

**Authors:** Xiaoming Lu, Flurin Babst, Eryuan Liang et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70893
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

AMO phase drives divergent treeline recruitment trends across the Northern Hemisphere — increasing at mid-latitudes, decreasing at high latitudes — via regional modulation of temperature, precipitation, and drought.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Large-scale atmospheric circulation modes like the AMO are known to influence climate globally, but how they drive tree recruitment dynamics at climatically marginal treeline populations across hemispheric scales is unresolved.
- **Method:** Field survey of 68,011 trees at 120 treeline sites across the Northern Hemisphere (1951–2010); linked recruitment chronologies to regional climate variables and AMO index via correlation and regression analyses.
- **Key result:** Recruitment increased on the Tibetan Plateau and Central China (mid-latitudes) but decreased in Scandinavia-Kola and Ural-Siberia (high latitudes); inverse recruitment patterns documented between mid-latitude Western North America and high-latitude Alaska-Northern Canada; AMO-driven regional warming and precipitation shifts mediated these patterns, with warming + adequate moisture promoting recruitment and warming-induced drought stress or low non-growing-season precipitation suppressing it.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that hemisphere-scale ocean-atmosphere variability is a key driver of treeline forest dynamics; essential for forecasting future forest range shifts and carbon feedbacks in climatically harsh environments under continued warming.
- **Caveats:** Observational design cannot fully disentangle AMO signal from other low-frequency climate modes (PDO, NAO); site coverage is uneven across the hemisphere; mechanisms linking AMO phases to local recruitment are inferred statistically, not experimentally.
