# [Synergistic effects of atmospheric circulation anomalies and land–atmosphere coupling on the 2022–2023 extreme drought in Southwest China](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6670)

**Authors:** Wanlin Yang, Ying Huang, Danqing Huang et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6670
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

A positive feedback loop between land–atmosphere coupling and circulation anomalies drove the most intense winter–spring drought in Southwest China since 1961.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Mechanisms sustaining the record-breaking winter 2022–spring 2023 drought in Southwest China (strongest meteorological drought since 1961) were poorly characterized, particularly the role of land–atmosphere feedbacks amplifying initial circulation-driven precipitation deficits.
- **Method:** Analysis of the 2022–2023 event using atmospheric reanalysis and observational data; examination of moisture transport anomalies, soil moisture percentiles, sensible heat flux, VPD, and surface diabatic heating feedbacks over Southwest China.
- **Key result:** Persistent large-scale circulation anomalies weakened southerly moisture transport and strengthened subsidence, driving a record precipitation deficit. When soil moisture fell below the 40th percentile, land–atmosphere coupling amplified sensible heat flux, raising surface temperature and VPD, which reinforced the circulation anomalies through surface diabatic heating — sustaining soil moisture deficits for months and culminating in the unprecedented April–May drought peak.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that crossing a soil moisture threshold (~40th percentile) activates a self-sustaining drought amplification loop; this mechanism must be represented in seasonal drought forecasting and LSM-coupled prediction systems for monsoon-influenced regions.
- **Caveats:** Single-event case study; quantitative attribution of each feedback component (circulation vs. land–atmosphere) not fully decomposed; generalizability to other drought events or regions unclear from abstract alone.
