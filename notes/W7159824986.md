# [Consistent Positive Associations Between Landscape Diversity and Crop Yields](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70894)

**Authors:** Katherine S. Nelson, Brennan Bean, Emily Burchfield
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70894
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Across U.S. crops and landscape diversity metrics, 87% of model specifications show positive yield associations; highly diverse landscapes average 2.7% higher yields than simplified ones.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Evidence on whether landscape diversity (heterogeneity of land cover) improves agricultural productivity is inconsistent, despite strong positive diversity–productivity relationships in natural ecosystems.
- **Method:** Mixture modeling across heterogeneous model specifications applied to U.S. crop yield data; systematic variation of crop types and landscape diversity operationalizations to test consistency of the diversity–yield relationship.
- **Key result:** 87% of specifications show positive landscape diversity–yield associations; highly diverse landscapes yield on average 2.7% more than highly simplified landscapes (credibility interval includes zero at 95% level); the relationship is non-saturating within current U.S. cropping systems.
- **Why it matters:** Non-saturating relationship implies that further landscape diversification could continue to benefit yields; supports policy promoting diverse agricultural landscapes without a clear ceiling effect in contemporary systems.
- **Caveats:** The 2.7% yield advantage is not significant at 95% credibility; results are observational and U.S.-centric; causal mechanisms underlying the positive association are not resolved; effect sizes vary across crops and diversity metrics.
