# [Terrestrial Runoff Divides the Central Arctic Ocean and Shapes the Biologically Relevant Marine Lightscape](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121318)

**Authors:** Håkon Sandven, David McKee, Rafael Gonçalves-Araujo et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121318
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Terrestrial CDOM carried by the Transpolar Drift raises light attenuation ~35% in the Amundsen Basin, inhibiting under-ice phytoplankton blooms and undermining ESM light-limitation estimates.

## Summary

- **Problem:** The Arctic Ocean's optical complexity — driven by terrestrial dissolved organic matter inputs — is poorly represented in Earth System Models, limiting projections of light limitation on marine ecosystems under rapid sea-ice decline.
- **Method:** In-situ optical observations across the western Eurasian Basin (summer 2022) measured CDOM absorption in Transpolar Drift (TPD) waters (Amundsen Basin) versus Atlantic-influenced waters (Nansen Basin); an irradiance model translated optical differences into ecological consequences.
- **Key result:** CDOM absorption in the TPD increases the diffuse attenuation coefficient by ~35% relative to Atlantic-influenced waters, with elevated CDOM persisting to 100 m depth; modeled consequences include delayed/inhibited under-ice phytoplankton blooms, altered zooplankton vertical behavior, and enhanced solar heating in the upper 10 m.
- **Why it matters:** Simplified light-attenuation schemes in current ESMs cannot capture this terrestrial-runoff-driven optical divide, causing systematic errors in modeled Arctic marine productivity and heat partitioning; as riverine discharge to the Arctic increases with warming, the bias will grow.
- **Caveats:** Single summer (2022) snapshot; spatial coverage limited to western Eurasian Basin; irradiance model impacts are scenario-dependent and not yet validated against biological field data.
