# [Soil type modulates stochastic assembly of methanotrophic communities and methane oxidation activity in paddy fields](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2026.117835)

**Authors:** Yalin Yin, Gengbo Jiang, Wanyi Jin et al.
**Venue:** Geoderma · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2026.117835
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Soil type (black, yellow, red) filters methanotroph communities and CH₄ oxidation potential in Chinese paddies via climate–nutrient gradients, with stochastic assembly increasing with mean annual temperature.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Continental-scale drivers of methanotrophic community assembly and methane oxidation potential in paddy soils are poorly understood, limiting CH₄ feedback predictions.
- **Method:** Surveyed methanotrophs across three major paddy soil types (black, yellow, red soils) along a broad climatic gradient in China; measured CH₄ oxidation potential and pmoA gene abundance; used network analysis and stochasticity indices to assess community assembly.
- **Key result:** CH₄ oxidation potential and pmoA abundance were highest in black soils (dominated by Type I Methylobacter); stochastic processes dominated assembly across all soils, with stochasticity increasing from black to red soils as MAT rose; the anaerobic methanotroph Methylomirabilis (NC10) was a keystone species specifically in yellow soil; functional enzyme abundance (pMMO, sMMO) matched measured oxidation potentials.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that soil type integrates climate and nutrient interactions to regulate CH₄ oxidation—soil-specific microbial mechanisms must be incorporated into models predicting CH₄ feedbacks from paddy ecosystems.
- **Caveats:** Continental-scale survey may conflate soil type effects with confounded land-management history; mechanistic causation between assembly stochasticity and oxidation rates is inferred, not experimentally manipulated.
