# [Climate Change Amplifies Rainfall Sensitivity to Deforestation in the Southern Amazon](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl119000)

**Authors:** Jie Zhang, Alice Catherine Hughes, Britaldo Soares‐Filho et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl119000
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Climate change drastically lowers the deforestation threshold for rainfall collapse in the Southern Amazon — from 50% under land-use change alone to just 10% under SSP5-8.5 by 2050.

## Summary

- **Problem:** The ~30–40% forest-loss tipping point for Amazon rainfall decline is well-established, but how climate change interacts with deforestation to shift that threshold is unknown.
- **Method:** Combined land-use change and climate projections (SSP1-2.6, SSP5-8.5) modeled through 2050 on 90 × 90 km grids across the Southern Amazon, simulating rainfall and temperature responses to joint forcing.
- **Key result:** Deforestation threshold for rainfall decline drops from 50% (land-use change alone) to 45% under SSP1-2.6 and to 10% under SSP5-8.5. Annual rainfall declines 1.7% under land-use change alone and up to 13.9% under SSP1-2.6; combined SSP5-8.5 warming reaches 1.4°C vs. 0.29°C from land-use change alone.
- **Why it matters:** Forest protection becomes disproportionately more critical under higher emissions because climate change erodes the moisture-recycling buffer that forests provide; the effective tipping point could be crossed at deforestation levels already observed in some subregions.
- **Caveats:** Threshold estimates are grid-scale (90 × 90 km) and model-dependent; only two SSP scenarios tested; interactions with fire regimes and aerosol feedbacks not explicitly represented.
