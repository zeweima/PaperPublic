# [Climate change-related effects on carbon emissions and net ammonification across lowland to upland soils from a high-latitude coastal ecosystem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10533-026-01336-w)

**Authors:** Jenna M. Ross, Karen H. Beard, Katharine C. Kelsey et al.
**Venue:** Biogeochemistry · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10533-026-01336-w
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Warming, flooding, and goose feces interact with soil-specific conditions to amplify or dampen CO₂, CH₄, and net ammonification in Arctic coastal soils, with flood effects reversing sign across landscape positions.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Warming, sea-level rise (flooding), and changing herbivory collectively alter C and N cycling in northern coastal soils, but their combined effects across landscape positions are poorly understood.
- **Method:** Full-factorial microcosm experiment using four soil types from the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta, Alaska — three coastal wetlands (varying flooding frequency and goose use) plus one upland tundra. Soils incubated 16 weeks at 10 °C or 18 °C, ± three short-term floods, ± goose feces; CO₂ and CH₄ measured weekly, net ammonification measured over the experiment.
- **Key result:** Higher temperature generally increased CO₂, CH₄, and net ammonification; feces increased CO₂. Flooding effects were soil-type-dependent: flooding suppressed CO₂ in the least-frequently flooded wetland and tundra but had no effect on the other two wetlands. Under warmer temperatures, flooding increased CH₄ in the most-frequently flooded wetland but decreased CH₄ in tundra. Flooding decreased net ammonification in the most-flooded wetland but increased it in tundra.
- **Why it matters:** Sea-level rise will shift flooding regimes across heterogeneous Arctic coastal landscapes; responses diverge by soil type, so landscape-position-specific parameterization is needed for C and N flux projections in high-latitude models.
- **Caveats:** Microcosm experiment (16 weeks); no intact soil structure or plant roots; short-term flood pulses may not capture sustained inundation; single geographic system (YK Delta).
