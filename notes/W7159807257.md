# [Asynchronous Landslide Seasonality Across the United States](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL121707)

**Authors:** L. V. Luna, B. B. Mirus, B. D. Collins et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL121707
**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Bayesian circular statistics applied to >55,000 U.S. landslides reveal strongly asynchronous regional seasonality — winter on the West Coast, summer in the Rockies, fall in Puerto Rico — with a credible 31-day shift toward a later season in western Washington from 1990 to 2020.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Continental-scale seasonal patterns of landslide activity are unquantified, limiting mid-range hazard outlooks and emergency preparedness planning.
- **Method:** Von Mises circular distributions fit via Bayesian MCMC (brms/Stan) to 56,257 landslides across 67 National Weather Service County Warning Areas (CWAs), drawn from the USGS Landslide Inventories compilation (991,271 observations, 55 inventories). Seasonality estimated for all failures combined and by movement type (slide, flow, fall). Von Mises regression assessed temporal trends in Seattle (SEW) and Portland (PQR) CWAs using records back to 1910.
- **Key result:** Clear regional asynchrony: concentrated winter seasons on the West Coast; spring in parts of the central and eastern U.S.; summer in the Rocky Mountain region and most arid CWAs; year-round in southeastern Alaska, Hawaii, coastal Mid-Atlantic, and New England; autumn in Puerto Rico. Slide vs. fall seasonality was apparently different in 27 of 35 CWAs (77%) with both types, but credibly distinguishable in only 2 CWAs (Juneau AK; Salt Lake City UT). In western Washington (SEW), a credible shift of 10 days per decade (95% HDI: 1.4–20 days/decade) toward a later season was detected 1990–2020, shifting mean season ~31 days (4.1–59) from early January to early February; no credible trend in western Oregon (PQR) or in longer SEW records (1910–2020, 1965–2020).
- **Why it matters:** Provides a quantitative, uncertainty-aware seasonal landslide probability map for U.S. emergency managers, complementing existing static susceptibility products; framework is directly transferable to other regions and other domain boundaries (watersheds, political units). Landslide seasonality only partially tracks Köppen-Geiger precipitation seasonality — transitions in the eastern U.S. align better with watershed boundaries, implicating topographic controls on triggering precipitation.
- **Caveats:** Single von Mises distribution per CWA cannot capture bimodal seasons; landslide counts (not intensity) drive the analysis; inventory reporting gaps and biases (e.g., Alaska winter access) may inflate uncertainty; trend analysis restricted to two Pacific Northwest CWAs with adequate record length and concentrated seasons; the 1990–2020 SEW trend may reflect decadal precipitation variability rather than a monotonic shift.
