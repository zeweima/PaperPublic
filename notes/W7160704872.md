# [Heterogeneous Responses of High‐Latitude Forest Productivity to Interannual Climate Variability](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121078)

**Authors:** Ke Liu, Junjie Liu, Yitong Yao et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-09 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121078
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

SIF-based analysis reveals high-latitude forest carbon uptake is primarily driven by early- and peak-season temperatures, but deciduous needleleaf forests show weak temperature sensitivity with strong water constraints.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How interannual climate variability drives growing-season productivity in high-latitude forests remains uncertain, especially across different plant functional types.
- **Method:** Satellite solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) observations used to quantify growing-season gross productivity across high-latitude forest plant functional types; climate sensitivity decomposed by season (early, peak, late) and by plant functional type.
- **Key result:** Warmer springs enhance carbon uptake in most regions, with moderate legacy declines later in the season that do not fully offset early-season gains. Deciduous needleleaf forests (DNF) show weaker temperature sensitivity and stronger water dependence than other forest types; temperature–carbon uptake relationship weakens or reverses in DNF under dry conditions.
- **Why it matters:** Highlights that a single temperature-sensitivity parameter is insufficient for high-latitude forest carbon cycle models; DNF water constraints must be represented explicitly to avoid overestimating warming-driven productivity gains.
- **Caveats:** SIF-based productivity estimates carry retrieval uncertainties; canopy structural effects are difficult to fully disentangle from physiological responses; limited growing-season length constrains interannual sample size.
