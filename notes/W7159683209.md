## TL;DR

No standard practice exists for monetizing coastal groundwater hazards; this commentary maps a hazard–exposure–vulnerability framework and roadmap to fill that gap.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Sea-level rise drives groundwater shoaling, salinization, and contaminant mobilization that damage subsurface infrastructure (foundations, roads, sewers, tunnels), yet no standardized workflow exists to quantify economic risk — unlike the mature literature on surface flood losses. Barnard et al. (2024) found >70% of southeastern US coastal residents exposed to shallow/emerging groundwater and ~$1 trillion [2024 USD] of property at risk; Abdelhafez et al. (2022) projected US$90 million/yr in foundation repair costs for Mobile Bay, AL alone by 2100.
- **Method:** Commentary/perspective. Proposes a risk framework adapted from flood risk analysis (hazard × exposure × vulnerability → monetized expected loss) with three vulnerability pathways: (1) depth-damage loss functions analogous to flood curves, extended to include salinity; (2) lifecycle cost analysis tracking accelerated asset replacement under rising groundwater; (3) accumulation management (property-tax-base exposure) for weakly constrained problems. Grounded by a stakeholder listening session with 33 Boston-area participants (Jan 2025) that catalogued 21 available datasets.
- **Key result:** No quantitative new results (commentary). Key illustrative numbers from cited work: pavement structural modifications can eliminate 80–90% of service-life reduction under 0.3 m sea-level rise, delaying inundation by 20 years (Knott et al., 2018). Salinity threshold for accelerated pipe corrosion is >3,500 ppm. Four knowledge gaps identified: (1) lack of long-term in-situ infrastructure corrosion monitoring; (2) unknown feedbacks between LID/green infrastructure and groundwater rise; (3) uncommunicated uncertainty in groundwater hazard predictions; (4) limited translational science bridging research and practice.
- **Why it matters:** Coastal adaptation decisions (where to extend sewers, when to retreat) currently ignore chronic groundwater hazards. Integrating groundwater into risk accounting — via loss functions, lifecycle costing, or digital twins linking real-time sensors, satellite land deformation, and ML — would expose billions in currently unpriced risk and enable evidence-based infrastructure prioritization. Calls for a Coastal Groundwater Observatory analogous to GLOSS (~2,000 tide gauges worldwide) as a near-term practical step.
- **Caveats:** Commentary only — no new empirical data or model validation. Proposed framework is aspirational; most vulnerability functions (depth-damage curves for groundwater, corrosion-salinity relationships at field scale) do not yet exist. Infrastructure asset databases (sewer networks, foundation depths) are sparse or non-standardized in most jurisdictions. Groundwater hazards span episodic to decadal timescales, complicating probabilistic annualized loss calculations standard in flood risk.
