# [Spatial scaling of drought impacts on forest mortality: when climate signals emerge from local noise](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111213)

**Authors:** Luiza Tymińska-Czabańska, Krzysztof Leszczyński, Piotr Janiec et al.
**Venue:** Agricultural and Forest Meteorology · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111213
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>

## TL;DR

Drought–mortality signal strength in Scots pine increases with spatial aggregation; multi-year legacy effects and spatial heterogeneity both carry ecologically meaningful information.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Drought-induced forest mortality is poorly predictable because local stochasticity obscures climatic signals; it is unclear at which spatial scale climatic water balance becomes a reliable mortality predictor.
- **Method:** Long-term mortality records for Scots pine (*Pinus sylvestris*) across three hierarchical spatial scales of forest management in Poland (local, intermediate, regional); climatic water balance as the main predictor; joint mean–dispersion models to capture heterogeneity.
- **Key result:** Drought–mortality relationship strength increases consistently with spatial aggregation as local noise diminishes; lagged climatic water balance effects reveal multi-year drought legacies; spatial heterogeneity carries meaningful ecological signal about forest vulnerability rather than being pure noise.
- **Why it matters:** A scale-aware framework improves mortality prediction and monitoring, directly informing adaptive forest management strategies for enhancing resilience under climate change.
- **Caveats:** Single species (Scots pine) in one country (Poland); generalizability to other forest types or regions unclear; specific quantitative effect sizes not reported in abstract.
