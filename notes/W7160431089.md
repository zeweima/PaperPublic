# [Introducing the Model Fidelity Metric (MFM) for robust and diagnostic land surface model evaluation](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2651-2026)

**Authors:** Z Z Wu, Zhongwang Wei, Xingjie Lu et al.
**Venue:** HESS (Hydrology and Earth System Sciences) · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2651-2026
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

MFM replaces NSE/KGE with robust statistics and information theory to give more reliable, diagnostic LSM performance scores.

## Summary
- **Problem:** NSE and KGE rely on moment-based statistics that fail with non-normal/skewed LSM outputs — they suffer error compensation, instability at low variance, and conflate magnitude with phase errors.
- **Method:** MFM combines three orthogonal performance dimensions in a Euclidean framework: (1) Accuracy via robust Normalized Mean Absolute p-Error (NMAEp) with a Phase Penalty Factor; (2) Variability via Scaled and Unscaled Shannon Entropy differences (SUSE); (3) Distribution similarity via Percentage of Histogram Intersection (PHI). Evaluated on synthetic experiments and the large-sample CAMELS dataset.
- **Key result:** MFM mitigates error compensation that misleads KGE and remains stable in low-variability regimes where NSE and KGE fail; approximation error in synthetic tests ~5% relative to the fully non-stationary benchmark.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a drop-in diagnostic alternative to NSE/KGE for LSM benchmarking that decouples phase from magnitude errors and decomposes overall skill into interpretable components.
- **Caveats:** Validated primarily on CAMELS (streamflow-centric); performance on non-hydrological LSM outputs (soil moisture, fluxes) not demonstrated in the abstract; additional computational cost relative to simple metrics not quantified.
