# [Regional Transport Constrains the Microphysical Response of Black Carbon to Short-Term Emission Controls](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c00143)

**Authors:** Shuo Ding, D G Liu, Yangzhou Wu et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c00143
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Regional transport dominates BC microphysical properties during emission controls, limiting the efficacy of short-term local measures.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Short-term emission controls (e.g., event-based restrictions) are assumed to improve BC-related air quality and climate properties, but the role of regional transport vs. local reductions in driving BC microphysical responses is poorly quantified.
- **Method:** Single-particle BC observations during the Asian Games emission controls; Random Forest with SHAP attribution to separate drivers; meteorological normalization to isolate emission vs. transport effects; counterfactual sensitivity experiments varying air mass origin.
- **Key result:** BC mass doubled immediately after controls were lifted, confirming rapid emission rebound. Meteorological normalization attributes ~40% of BC mass decrease during controls to emission reductions; changes in BC absorption and CCN activity were strongly modulated by regional transport, with transport effects comparable to or exceeding local emission reductions. Counterfactual experiments show air mass origin alone can substantially alter BC microphysical properties under identical emission conditions.
- **Why it matters:** Short-term local controls effectively suppress BC microphysical properties (mixing state, optical properties, hygroscopicity, CCN activity), but efficacy is largely constrained by regional transport — regional coordination of emission controls is needed to achieve reliable climate and air quality co-benefits.
- **Caveats:** Single case study (Asian Games); results may not generalize to other emission control contexts or regions with different transport regimes; ML-SHAP attribution is correlative, not mechanistic.
