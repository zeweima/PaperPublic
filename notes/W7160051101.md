## TL;DR

Multi-model intercomparison of intercropping simulators reveals how current crop models conceptualise and partition aboveground and belowground resource sharing between species.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Intercropping models differ widely in how they represent resource competition and facilitation between co-cultivated species, hampering prediction of yield advantages and limiting adoption by modellers.
- **Method:** Conceptual intercomparison engaging model developers and expert users across multiple established crop simulation frameworks; focus on resource sharing (light, water, nitrogen) algorithms.
- **Key result:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Why it matters:** Identifying structural divergences among intercropping models guides prioritisation of process improvements and provides a benchmark for future quantitative intercomparison exercises.
- **Caveats:** Conceptual (not quantitative) comparison; no common dataset evaluated; conclusions depend on self-reported model descriptions from developer–user teams.
