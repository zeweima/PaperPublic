# [Drivers of spatiotemporal variability of river water quality](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae692c)

**Authors:** Linus S. Schauer, James W. Jawitz, Matthew Cohen et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae692c
**Score:** 8/10
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Landscape source configuration drives spatial water-quality patterns while local hydro-climatic drivers govern temporal variability, with opposite implications for monitoring design.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Unclear what mechanisms govern spatial vs. temporal variability of river solute concentrations across scales, and how this should inform monitoring allocation.
- **Method:** Stochastic model applied to synthetic river networks comprising hundreds of subcatchments; simulated daily discharge and solute concentration time series systematically varying hydro-climatic regimes and spatial configuration of mean solute source concentrations; hydraulic routing with depth-dependent in-stream processing.
- **Key result:** Spatial variability is predominantly determined by landscape source configuration (long autocorrelation scales); temporal variability is predominantly determined by local drivers with short spatial correlation lengths. High landscape heterogeneity produces persistent spatial patterns through time; high temporal variability does not produce synchronized patterns across monitoring sites.
- **Why it matters:** Spatially distributed but temporally sparse sampling is sufficient to characterize spatial patterns in heterogeneous landscapes; temporal dynamics require dense time-series at individual sites — directly guides monitoring network design under limited resources.
- **Caveats:** Synthetic network approach; results depend on parameterization of source configuration and in-stream processing; applicability to specific real catchments requires empirical validation.
