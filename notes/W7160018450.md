# [Optimization of Parsimonious and Synchronized Discharge and Dissolved Organic Carbon Models Across Catchment Scales](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040487)

**Authors:** Juan Pesántez, Christian Birkel, Gabriel Gaona et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040487
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Simultaneously optimizing discharge and DOC in a parsimonious model across nested catchments (7–480 km²) shows antecedent precipitation >45 days drives DOC dynamics at all scales.

## Summary

- **Problem:** DOC export and discharge are jointly controlled by hydrometeorological drivers, but most models treat them separately; how these controls scale across catchment sizes is poorly quantified.
- **Method:** Parsimonious model co-optimizing discharge and DOC concentrations using 14+ years of 15-min hydrological data plus weekly–monthly DOC measurements at three nested catchments (7, 170, 480 km²) in a temperate snow-dominated forested system (New Hampshire, USA). KGE used as performance metric.
- **Key result:** KGE ≈ 0.60 for both discharge and DOC at all three scales. Shallow soil storage and antecedent precipitation >45 days are dominant DOC controls at every scale; antecedent precipitation effect grows with catchment area, while episodic DOC pulses are more frequent at the smallest scale due to faster source activation.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that simultaneous hydro-biogeochemical optimization outperforms decoupled approaches and that the same parsimonious structure transfers across an order-of-magnitude range in catchment area — useful for predicting DOC export under climate change or land-use shifts.
- **Caveats:** Single catchment complex (one climate/vegetation type); KGE ~0.60 leaves substantial unexplained variance; weekly–monthly DOC sampling limits constraint on high-frequency dynamics; model cannot yet represent in-stream processing or deep groundwater contributions.
