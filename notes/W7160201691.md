# [Gene‐Informed Modeling of Denitrification Process in Groundwater Through Dynamic Flux Balance Analysis and Deep Learning](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026wr043411)

**Authors:** Heng Dai, Yiyu Zhang, Yamin Deng et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2026wr043411
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Two gene-informed models — a DFBA-reactive transport model and a deep-learning surrogate — outperform conventional geochemistry-only RTMs for simulating groundwater denitrification by explicitly linking microbial genomic data to solute transformation.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Conventional reactive transport models (RTMs) of subsurface denitrification ignore microbial genomic information, limiting mechanistic accuracy and predictive performance for NO₃⁻ depletion and NO₂⁻ transients under dynamic surface-water/groundwater exchange.
- **Method:** Two complementary frameworks: (1) dynamic flux balance analysis (DFBA) coupled to PFLOTRAN RTM, resolving genome-scale metabolic networks for pathway-explicit denitrification; (2) a deep-learning model trained on environmental covariates and functional gene abundances to emulate biogeochemical kinetics at greatly reduced cost. Validated against controlled batch and column experiments with alternating surface-water/groundwater flow regimes.
- **Key result:** Both gene-informed models reproduce observed NO₃⁻ depletion and NO₂⁻ transients and outperform the conventional geochemistry-only RTM; the deep-learning surrogate runs at orders-of-magnitude lower computational cost while retaining accuracy suitable for multi-scenario analyses.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that microbial genomic information substantially improves hydro-biogeochemical simulations; provides a scalable, genetically informed modeling paradigm extensible to other contaminant and redox systems in the subsurface.
- **Caveats:** Validated only in controlled laboratory (batch and column) experiments; performance in heterogeneous field-scale aquifers with sparse genomic sampling is untested. Functional gene abundances must be measured, limiting applicability where metagenomics data are unavailable.
