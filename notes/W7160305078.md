# [Intensifying Aridity Undermines the Role of Soil Biodiversity in Supporting Ecosystem Stability](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70903)

**Authors:** Jiejun Qi, Chunling Liang, Chengwei Zhang et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70903
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Increasing aridity progressively decouples soil biodiversity from ecosystem stability by collapsing microbial network complexity and shifting microbial life history toward stress tolerance.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Whether and how aridity modulates the positive biodiversity–stability relationship in soil ecosystems is unknown despite relevance to dryland management under climate change.
- **Method:** Integrated a 265-site dryland agricultural field survey along a 3800 km east-west transect in China with a global meta-dataset of 996 sites across six continents; ecosystem stability quantified from 11-year NDVI (2012–2022); metagenomic analyses of microbial life history strategies.
- **Key result:** Soil biodiversity positively associates with ecosystem stability, but this relationship weakens significantly as aridity increases; declining microbial network complexity with aridity partially explains the decoupling; metagenomics show microbial strategies shift toward stress tolerance at the expense of growth yield and resource acquisition under higher aridity.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that climate-driven aridity actively erodes a key ecological insurance mechanism (biodiversity buffering); microbial network complexity and life history strategies are identified as mechanistic predictors of biodiversity–stability relationships relevant to dryland conservation and carbon cycle modeling.
- **Caveats:** NDVI as a stability proxy captures aboveground productivity but may miss belowground or functional stability dimensions; transect and meta-analysis data are observational, so causal attribution of aridity effects is inferred rather than experimentally established.
