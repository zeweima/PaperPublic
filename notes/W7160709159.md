**Venue:** JGR Machine Learning and Computation · **Date:** 2026-05-09 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jh001011

## TL;DR

ConvLSTM-based "Smart-NINT" emulator replaces explicit tracer transport in NASA GISS ModelE, achieving R² ~0.80 for surface black carbon while restoring meteorological feedbacks lost in static climatologies.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Full coupling of atmospheric composition (tracer advection, removal) in ESMs is computationally prohibitive; the current workaround—NASA GISS ModelE's Non-Interactive Tracer (NINT) method using pre-calculated monthly climatologies—eliminates dynamic meteorology–chemistry feedbacks entirely.
- **Method:** Neural network emulators trained on 2 years (1950–1951) of fully coupled ModelE output to approximate advection and removal terms governing tracer evolution; surface-level black carbon from wildfire sources as test case. Architectures compared include ConvLSTM (spatiotemporal), standard LSTM, and convolutional approaches.
- **Key result:** All architectures reached average R² ~0.80; ConvLSTM outperformed others by jointly exploiting spatial and temporal dependencies.
- **Why it matters:** A "Smart-NINT" layer could restore meteorologically responsive composition fields in ESMs at a fraction of the cost of full tracer transport, directly enabling longer or higher-resolution climate projections.
- **Caveats:** Training window is only 2 years and single tracer (surface black carbon/wildfire); generalizability to other aerosol species, 3-D column profiles, and longer climate periods is untested. R² ~0.80 may be insufficient for applications sensitive to tail events.
