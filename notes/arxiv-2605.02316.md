# [Open-access model for detecting openly dumped dispersed municipal solid waste from crowdsourced UAV imagery in Sub-Saharan Africa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02316)

**Authors:** Steffen Knoblauch, Ram Kumar Muthusamy, Luis M. A. Bettencourt et al.

**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Deep learning on crowdsourced UAV imagery detects dispersed illegal dumping across 29 regions in 10 Sub-Saharan African countries, linking hotspots to population density and infrastructure deficits.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Dispersed informal solid waste dumping in rapidly urbanizing Sub-Saharan Africa lacks scalable spatial monitoring tools; fine-resolution data are scarce and manual annotation is costly.
- **Method:** Deep learning model trained on manually annotated UAV image tiles; evaluated across 29 regions in 10 countries covering diverse environmental contexts; model and weights released open-access for use with crowdsourced UAV imagery.
- **Key result:** Model achieves "excellent performance" in waste detection across all study regions (quantitative metrics not reported in abstract); waste accumulation correlates most strongly with population density and local infrastructure access deficits, weakly with broader regional development indices.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a ready-to-use, no-expertise-required tool for municipalities and local mapping communities to convert UAV imagery into actionable waste management insights; identifies waterway-adjacent hotspots where dumping amplifies flood and public health risks.
- **Caveats:** Quantitative accuracy metrics not available from abstract; generalizability beyond Sub-Saharan Africa unverified; relies on continued crowdsourced UAV data collection infrastructure.
