# [Attributing Upper-Tropospheric Warm Biases in CMIP6 Models to Ice Cloud-Radiation Interaction Deficiencies Over Tropical Oceans](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL120130)

**Authors:** J.-L. F. Li, Wei-Liang Lee, Kuan-Man Xu et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL120130

**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

CMIP6 models that treat falling ice and cloud ice radiatively as separate categories (SON2) reduce upper-tropospheric warm biases by 1–2 K vs. models that omit or lump falling-ice radiative effects.

## Summary

- **Problem:** CMIP6 models persistently overestimate upper-tropospheric temperatures over tropical/subtropical oceans by 1–2 K at 400–200 hPa; the role of frozen-hydrometeor radiative parameterization in driving this bias was unquantified.
- **Method:** 26 CMIP6 historical simulations (1980–2014) grouped into three subsets — NOS (no falling-ice radiative effects, 13 models), SON1 (combined cloud+falling ice via GA71 scheme, 6 models), SON2 (separate prognostic falling ice via MG2 scheme, 8 models) — benchmarked against GNSS radio occultation (2002–2014), Microwave Limb Sounder (300 hPa, 2005–2010), and AIRS Obs4MIPs v2.1 (2002–2016) over tropical/subtropical Pacific oceans. CESM2 paired on/off falling-ice sensitivity experiments (historical, 1850–2014) isolate the mechanism.
- **Key result:** SON2 mean absolute bias at 200 hPa = 1.21 K vs. 1.52 K (NOS) and 1.83 K (SON1) relative to radio occultation; at 300 hPa = 0.83 K vs. 1.08 K and 1.41 K. SON2 reduces TAA biases ~26% relative to NOS and ~51% relative to SON1. SON1 biases are comparable to or worse than NOS despite including falling-ice effects, because the GA71 single-diameter unified treatment fails to replicate the cloud-radiation-circulation feedback chain. CESM2-NOS (falling ice off) produces widespread warm biases up to 1.5 K vs. radio occultation; CESM2-SON reduces those biases across all three pressure levels (200, 300, 400 hPa, p < 0.05). The mechanism: omitting falling-ice radiative effects weakens trade-wind stress, warms SSTs (El Nino-like), enhances mid/high cloud and condensational heating above the PBL, driving upper-tropospheric warming consistent with moist-adiabatic adjustment.
- **Why it matters:** SON2-type models are underrepresented in CMIP6, so ensemble means understate falling-ice importance; adopting separate cloud-ice/falling-ice radiative treatment (MG2-class schemes) in CMIP7 is a concrete path to reducing persistent upper-tropospheric bias and improving climate-projection reliability.
- **Caveats:** SON2 still shows non-negligible cold anomalies over western Pacific at 300 hPa and warm anomalies over eastern Pacific at 850 hPa; subgroup differences may also reflect resolution, aerosol forcing, and air-sea coupling differences, not only microphysics; MG/MG2 schemes are restricted to stratiform clouds, leaving convective hydrometeor radiative effects unrepresented; causes of SON1 underperformance require dedicated FIREs on/off experiments within SON1 models.
