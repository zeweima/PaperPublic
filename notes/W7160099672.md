# [Tracking Water Dynamics of a Temperate Forest Under Drought and Non‐Drought Conditions Using Active and Passive Microwave and Optical Remote Sensing](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009240)

**Authors:** Florian M. Hellwig, Anke Fluhrer, Clémence Dubois et al.
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009240

**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Multi-frequency microwave remote sensing distinguishes drought onset (X/C-band VOD) from legacy drought effects (L-band VOD) in a temperate beech forest, with Sentinel-1 backscatter switching between soil moisture and leaf biomass sensitivity across phenological seasons.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Forest water status and ecohydrological resilience under multi-year drought are hard to monitor continuously; it is unclear which remote sensing signals capture drought onset vs. cumulative structural damage in trees.
- **Method:** Six-plus years of multi-sensor time series over a protected German beech forest, integrating Sentinel-1 C-band SAR backscatter (γ⁰), AMSR2 X- and C-band vegetation optical depth (VOD), and SMAP L-band VOD across the 2018–2020 multi-year drought. Correlation and sensitivity analyses used environmental and newly developed hydrological selection strategies to isolate signal drivers.
- **Key result:** During leaf-on, wet conditions Sentinel-1 γVH correlates strongly with leaf area index (r = −0.94); during leaf-off, dry conditions it switches to topsoil moisture sensitivity (r = 0.91). X- and C-band RWC VOD capture fast water content changes in leaves/twigs (drought onset signal); L-band RWC VOD records slower changes in branches/trunks, reflecting legacy effects of repetitive droughts.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that sensor frequency determines whether forest water stress monitoring reflects canopy phenology, topsoil moisture, or deep structural drought memory — critical for selecting the right sensor for operational drought early-warning in forests.
- **Caveats:** Single protected beech forest site in Germany; generalization to other forest types, canopy structures, or drier climates is unclear. Coarse spatial resolution of passive microwave sensors limits site-level inference.
