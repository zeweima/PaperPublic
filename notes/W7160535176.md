# [Temperature-dependent kinetics of microbial particulate organic matter reduction and its regulatory effect on CH4 emissions in peatlands](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2026.117834)

**Authors:** Hong-Yan Wang, Xin Yan, Ruo-Yi Zhen et al.
**Venue:** Geoderma · **Date:** 2026-05-08 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2026.117834
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Peatland POM acts as a CH4 gateway: microbial POM reduction follows pseudo-first-order kinetics with Q10 ~1.8–2.5, and CH4 production only begins after ~90% of accessible POM electron-accepting capacity is consumed.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Temperature sensitivity of microbial reduction of particulate organic matter (POM) as an electron acceptor in peatlands, and how it regulates the timing of methanogenesis, are poorly constrained.
- **Method:** Anoxic incubation of peat from five poor fens in China at 15°C and 30°C; POM electron-accepting capacity (EAC_POM) tracked over time via mediated electrochemical reduction (MER); CH4 formation monitored in Tibetan Plateau peat soils.
- **Key result:** EAC_POM declined exponentially (pseudo-first-order, R² = 0.86–0.99); 58 ± 12% of electrochemically reducible EAC_POM (120–860 µmol e⁻ g⁻¹ peat C) was microbially accessible. Rate constants at 30°C (0.08–0.28 d⁻¹) were ~3× those at 15°C; Q10 ranged 1.8–2.5. CH4 formation became significant only after ~90% of microbially available EAC_POM was depleted; higher temperature accelerated POM reduction and advanced the CH4 onset.
- **Why it matters:** Quantifies POM's "gatekeeping" role in peatland CH4 emissions and shows warming will shorten the lag before methanogenesis — with direct implications for peatland carbon feedback under climate change.
- **Caveats:** Five sites, all poor fens in China; temperature range limited to 15–30°C; POM accessibility fraction assumed invariant with temperature, which may not hold under extreme warming or drying; field validation needed.
