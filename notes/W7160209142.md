# [Bridging the Energy Balance Gap in Eddy‐Covariance Measurements: Insights From Standardized Network Data](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70892)

**Authors:** Giacomo Nicolini, David Durden, Luca Di Fiore et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70892
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Comprehensive energy accounting across 84 ICOS/NEON sites improved energy balance closure by 16% on average (up to 40% at individual sites), but a persistent residual gap remains tied to fundamental physical processes.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Eddy-covariance (EC) energy balance closure (EBC) typically reaches only 60–80%, but the relative contributions of missing storage terms, data quality, and flux processing choices have not been cleanly separated.
- **Method:** Standardized ICOS and NEON datasets from 84 sites; systematic addition of soil heat flux, soil/air/biomass storage terms, and photosynthetic energy demand, combined with strict turbulent-flux quality filtering.
- **Key result:** Full energy accounting + quality filtering improved EBC by 16% on average; site-specific gains reached 40%. A persistent residual imbalance survives all methodological fixes, pointing to unaccounted physical processes rather than data handling errors.
- **Why it matters:** Defines a practical upper bound on how much EBC improvement is achievable through better methods, and argues the residual gap must be explicitly bounded rather than absorbed into correction schemes — directly affecting carbon, water, and climate flux estimates from flux networks.
- **Caveats:** Analysis constrained to ICOS and NEON sites; site-level variability in gains is large (0–40%), so network-mean improvement may not apply to individual study sites; root causes of residual imbalance remain unresolved.
