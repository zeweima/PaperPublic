## TL;DR

Cryptic sulfide-driven autotrophic denitrification is a quantifiable, previously overlooked N₂O source in seasonally hypoxic river-reservoir systems.

## Summary

- **Problem:** N₂O production pathways in oligotrophic aquatic systems are poorly constrained; the role of cryptic sulfide-driven autotrophic denitrification (sulfide oxidation coupled to nitrate reduction) has not been quantified in river-reservoir contexts experiencing seasonal hypoxia.
- **Method:** <unclear from abstract> — study conducted in a seasonally hypoxic river-reservoir system; microbial community analysis included; likely isotopic or incubation-based quantification of N₂O pathways.
- **Key result:** Cryptic sulfide-driven autotrophic denitrification contributes measurably to N₂O production; provides novel insights into microbial community interactions in oligotrophic aquatic systems (magnitude unclear from abstract).
- **Why it matters:** Identifies an under-characterized N₂O source that could shift greenhouse gas budgets for impounded river systems; relevant to reservoir management and N₂O emission inventories.
- **Caveats:** Findings from a single river-reservoir system; generalizability to other aquatic systems and hypoxia regimes unclear; quantitative contribution not recoverable from the available abstract fragment.
