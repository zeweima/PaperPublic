# [Physics-based models outperform AI weather forecasts of record-breaking extremes](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec1433)

**Authors:** Zhongwei Zhang, Erich Fischer, Jakob Zscheischler et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec1433
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

ECMWF's physics-based HRES consistently beats five state-of-the-art AI models at forecasting record-breaking heat, cold, and wind extremes across nearly all lead times.

## Summary

- **Problem:** AI weather models (GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, Fuxi) have matched or exceeded numerical weather prediction on standard benchmarks, but their reliability at extrapolating to unprecedented record-breaking extremes — the events most critical for early warning — is unknown.
- **Method:** Compared HRES against GraphCast, GraphCast operational, Pangu-Weather, Pangu-Weather operational, and Fuxi on record-breaking heat, cold, and wind events; evaluated forecast errors and bias in predicted frequency and intensity of record exceedances across lead times.
- **Key result:** AI model errors are consistently larger than HRES for all three extreme types at nearly all lead times; AI models underestimate frequency and intensity of record-breaking events, underpredicting hot records and overpredicting cold records, with errors growing with record exceedance magnitude.
- **Why it matters:** AI weather models are increasingly proposed for high-stakes applications (early warning, disaster management); this benchmark reveals a systematic extrapolation failure precisely where correct forecasts matter most, in a warming climate that increasingly produces record events.
- **Caveats:** Study covers the five named AI models only; results may not generalize to future architectures or hybrid physics–AI systems; analysis is conditional on available record-breaking events in the evaluation period, which may be limited.
