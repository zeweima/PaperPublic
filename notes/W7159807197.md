# [Cerium isotopes unveil hydrogenetic Fe-Mn encrustation occurring throughout from the oxygen minimum zone to the deep pacific](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee2813)

**Authors:** Wenshuai Li, Ryoichi Nakada, Hajime Obata et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee2813
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Ce isotopes link Mn oxide precipitation to the oxygen minimum zone, revising where and how Fe-Mn crusts form in the Pacific.

## Summary

- **Problem:** The depth range and mechanisms controlling hydrogenetic Fe-Mn crust formation — and the coupling between marine Mn and Ce cycles — were poorly constrained, particularly across the oxygen minimum zone (OMZ).
- **Method:** Ce and Mn profiles measured in seawater and Fe-Mn crusts across the OMZ in the Northwest Pacific Ocean; Ce isotope fractionation quantified between crusts and ambient seawater to link marine Mn and Ce cycles.
- **Key result:** Ce is closely associated with Mn oxides in Fe-Mn crusts; Ce isotope fractionation between crusts and seawater is quantified; results support Mn oxide precipitation initiating within the OMZ and persisting into the deep ocean.
- **Why it matters:** Revises the conceptual model of Fe-Mn crust genesis — OMZ dynamics play a previously underappreciated role in controlling hydrogenetic encrustation depth range and the coupled marine Mn-Ce elemental cycles.
- **Caveats:** Abstract is truncated; geographic scope limited to Northwest Pacific — applicability to other ocean basins unclear from available text.
