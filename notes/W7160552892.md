# [Climate-induced range shifts support local plant diversity but don't reduce extinction risk](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea1676)

**Authors:** Junna Wang, Brunno F. Oliveira, Frances C. Moore et al.
**Venue:** Science · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea1676
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Range shifts will increase local plant richness over 28% of land but won't prevent 7–16% of species losing >90% of their range by 2100.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Biodiversity forecasts rely on oversimplified dispersal assumptions (none, unlimited, or uniform), inflating uncertainty in extinction risk and conservation planning under climate change.
- **Method:** Species-specific range shift velocities modeled from BioShifts (14,488 records, 6,579 plant species); overlaid with ensemble habitat suitability maps (6.8 M occurrence records, 2 habitat models, 10 GCMs) at 8×8 km resolution for 18% of known vascular plants under 4 SSP scenarios for 2081–2100.
- **Key result:** 7–16% of modeled species projected to lose >90% of their range; 70–80% of losses driven by habitat disappearance rather than dispersal limitation. Range shifts increase local richness across 28% of land surface; tropics and subtropics (35°S–35°N) maintain latitudinally averaged richness; high latitudes (>50°N) suffer sharp declines as warming outpaces dispersal.
- **Why it matters:** Dispersal limitations are secondary to habitat loss as the extinction driver, shifting conservation priority toward protecting climate refugia and expanding ex situ networks (seed banks, botanic gardens) over assisted migration corridors; high-latitude connectivity gaps remain a regional exception where assisted movement could help.
- **Caveats:** Covers only ~18% of vascular plant species; range shift velocities extrapolated from observed records that may not represent all taxa; land-use change and biotic interactions not fully integrated; climate model spread adds uncertainty at regional scales.
