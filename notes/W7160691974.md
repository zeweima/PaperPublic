# [Field Evidence of Carbon and Nitrogen Stabilization Through Mineral‐Associated Organic Matter Formation in Coastal Wetland Soils](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009429)

**Authors:** Anthony J. Mirabito, Gabriel Pereira, Nia Hurst et al.
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009429
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

MAOM comprises 38–66% of total C and >60% of total N in coastal wetland soils, with created wetlands showing higher C/N stabilization efficiency than natural ones despite lower absolute MAOM concentrations.

## Summary

- **Problem:** MAOM dynamics are well-characterized in upland soils but quantification in coastal wetland soils — critical for long-term C/N burial — remains limited.
- **Method:** Compared MAOM concentrations and stabilization mechanisms across two natural coastal wetlands (one organic, one mineral) and one created wetland in Apalachicola, FL; analyzed physicochemical vs. biochemical protection indicators across 50-cm soil profiles.
- **Key result:** MAOM-C was highest in the natural organic wetland (66–108 mg g⁻¹) and lowest in the created wetland (16–33 mg g⁻¹), but the created wetland stabilized 44–65% of total C and 68–84% of total N within MAOM — higher fractions than the natural wetlands. MAOM fraction increased with depth (physicochemical protection), while lignin/C:N indicators of biochemical protection were weak. MAOM pool was better predicted by organic soil properties than by mineral properties.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates MAOM is a dominant SOM pool in coastal wetlands and a key driver of long-term C/N burial; created wetlands can achieve high stabilization efficiency relatively quickly, supporting wetland restoration as a carbon sequestration strategy.
- **Caveats:** Two-site comparison limits generalizability; mechanisms driving the organic-property-over-mineral-property prediction warrant further investigation; single region (Florida) may not represent global coastal wetland diversity.
