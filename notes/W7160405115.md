# [Decadal East Asian monsoon anomalies and implications for societal conflicts](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee1648)

**Authors:** Kan Zhao, Yijia Liang, Changchun Huang et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee1648
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Annual-resolution speleothem record (1787–2007 CE) links five East Asian megadrought extremes to reduced solar output and ocean-atmosphere feedbacks, with the 1850s flood-drought alternation substantially fueling the Taiping Rebellion.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Mechanisms and societal impacts of decadal-scale East Asian summer monsoon variability are poorly constrained due to limited instrumental records.
- **Method:** Annual-resolution speleothem proxy record from China spanning 1787–2007 CE, combined with climate model simulations; monsoon extremes identified and compared against historical societal records.
- **Key result:** Persistent monsoon weakening since the end of the Little Ice Age, with five megadrought extremes triggered by diminished solar output and amplified by Atlantic and Pacific ocean-atmosphere processes; a marked flood-drought alternation in the 1850s substantially contributed to the Taiping Rebellion (1851–1864 CE).
- **Why it matters:** Establishes quantitative linkage between hydroclimate variability and large-scale societal conflict, showing crop failure and overpopulation pressure convert monsoon anomalies into social unrest — relevant for projecting climate-conflict risks under future warming.
- **Caveats:** Single speleothem site limits spatial generalization; causal attribution of societal collapse to climate relies on correlation with historical records; model simulations may not fully capture ocean-atmosphere feedbacks at decadal timescales.
