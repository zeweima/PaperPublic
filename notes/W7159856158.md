# [Prevalent Greening Conceals the Forgone Ecological Potential of Forest Loss in Southeast Asia](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL121593)

**Authors:** Weiqing Zhao, Maggie Church, Zaichun Zhu et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL121593
**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Southeast Asia gained net greenness (+5.47% EVI, +12.47% NIRv) 2001–2022 yet forest loss forfeited 16.12% of potential greenness gains and 6.06% of productivity gains relative to stable-forest counterfactuals.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Widespread forest loss (25.18% of SEA forest extent by GLC_FCS30D) co-occurred with regional net greening driven by CO2 fertilization and agricultural intensification, masking the true ecological cost of deforestation. Cloud cover and multi-sensor inconsistencies further hindered attribution at landscape scale.
- **Method:** Cloud-robust 30-m vegetation record (EVI, NIRv) for 2001–2022 built from Landsat 5/7/8 harmonized against MODIS via phenology-informed Random Forest Regressors; 200,000 randomly sampled pixels across SEA. Propensity-score matching (1-to-3 nearest neighbor) paired forest-loss pixels with stable-forest controls matched on topography, climate, pre-disturbance vegetation trends, country, ecoregion, and forest type to construct counterfactual trajectories.
- **Key result:** Despite regional net EVI increase of 5.47% and NIRv increase of 12.47%, PSM counterfactual analysis shows forest conversion created lasting deficits of ~16.12% in greenness and ~6.06% in productivity relative to what intact forests would have achieved under the same environmental trends. Forest-to-cropland transitions caused the largest immediate drops (EVI: -8.68 to -10.40%; NIRv: -12.48 to -14.50%); forest-to-shrubland transitions showed smaller initial losses but persistent divergence. Cambodia was the largest hotspot (EVI deficit 72.10%; NIRv deficit 21.78%). Post-2015 deceleration in forest loss rates recovered 1.49% greenness and 0.94% productivity potential across the region.
- **Why it matters:** Surface greening metrics used to infer ecosystem health or carbon uptake can be deeply misleading in regions with concurrent land-use change and environmental change; intact primary forests are irreplaceable for carbon, water, and habitat functions. Policy-driven forest-loss deceleration moderates but does not reverse the ecological debt.
- **Caveats:** Ecological deficits are likely underestimated because stable-forest controls may include subtly degraded pixels (selective logging, edge effects); plantation-distribution map frozen at 2021 slightly misclassifies early natural-forest loss (~4.84–7.45% of total loss area); PSM cannot fully disentangle relative contributions of forest-loss intensity, post-conversion land use, and background climate at the national scale.
