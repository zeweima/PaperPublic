# [HONO emission from marine algae](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeb8486)

**Authors:** Hengqing Shen, Xuelian Zhong, Yujiao Zhu et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeb8486
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Marine algae emit HONO via abiotic photodecomposition at fluxes comparable to soils, boosting atmospheric OH and ozone production.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Marine HONO sources are poorly constrained; prior work focused on nocturnal terrestrial/soil emissions, leaving coastal photochemistry budgets incomplete.
- **Method:** Field observations of HONO during *Ulva prolifera* green tide events (daytime tidal-cycle correlation); laboratory chamber experiments with *U. prolifera* and *Sargassum* under controlled irradiation; incorporation of measured fluxes into atmospheric chemistry models.
- **Key result:** Measured HONO fluxes 1.08×10⁻⁷ to 2.31×10⁻⁶ mol m⁻² hr⁻¹, comparable to soil emissions and 2–3 orders of magnitude above marine NO fluxes; model runs with this source substantially elevated HONO, OH, and ozone concentrations.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies a previously unrecognized, light-driven abiotic pathway distinct from microbial soil HONO production; with algal blooms intensifying under eutrophication and warming, this source is expected to grow in atmospheric importance, affecting oxidation of climate-relevant gases.
- **Caveats:** Flux measurements are from chamber experiments and one coastal green-tide setting; global extrapolation depends on uncertain bloom extent and species-specific emission factors; model sensitivity to other HONO sinks not fully described in abstract.
