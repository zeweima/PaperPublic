# [Transient water bodies drive runoff variability across space and time in the Pan-Arctic Ob River Basin](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae692e)

**Authors:** Guojing Gan, Jinglu Wu, Ruibiao Yang et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae692e
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Seasonal (transient) water body extent explains >60% of Budyko n-parameter variability in the Ob Basin, enabling decadal runoff prediction with R² > 0.9 across sub-basins.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Spatiotemporal coupling between surface water inundation dynamics and runoff generation under permafrost thaw in the Pan-Arctic Ob River Basin (supplying ~1/6 of Arctic Ocean freshwater) is poorly understood; traditional permafrost thaw impact modeling is challenged.
- **Method:** Landsat imagery (1989–2017) over 13 watersheds used to map water inundation frequency (WIF); permanent water (WIF > 75%) and seasonal/transient water (25% ≤ WIF ≤ 75%) distinguished; Budyko framework applied to decompose decadal runoff change into climate vs. land-surface (n-parameter) drivers.
- **Key result:** Normalized seasonal water area explains >60% of variability in the Budyko n coefficient; spatiotemporal symmetry between n and seasonal water extent yields decadal runoff prediction R² > 0.9 across sub-basins; seasonal water area expanded 1989–2017, enhancing subsurface recharge and amplifying runoff under permafrost degradation.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a simple, remotely sensed emergent constraint for runoff modeling in Arctic regions where permafrost complicates process-based LSMs; highlights that transient (not permanent) water bodies are the dominant hydrological control.
- **Caveats:** Analysis spans only 1989–2017 and 13 sub-basins within a single basin; Budyko framework assumes steady-state conditions that may not hold under rapid permafrost change; subsurface recharge mechanisms inferred rather than directly measured.
