# [Dust storms: Hidden drivers of extreme rainfall and global precipitation shifts](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw6864)

**Authors:** Yuzhi Liu, Weiqi Tang, Tianbin Shao et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw6864
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Dust storms boost 7-day post-storm precipitation by up to 9.6 mm globally and, in high-anthropogenic-aerosol regions, shift rainfall from light to heavy events.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Dust aerosols are typically studied for their harmful effects; their role as drivers of extreme precipitation at global scale and their interaction with anthropogenic aerosols was unquantified.
- **Method:** Global observations of post-dust-storm accumulated precipitation (7-day window, dust vs. dust-free conditions) combined with numerical simulations examining dust particles as ice nuclei and their interaction with anthropogenic aerosol regimes.
- **Key result:** 7-day accumulated precipitation after dust storms exceeds dust-free baselines by up to 9.6 mm. In regions with elevated anthropogenic aerosols, dust suppresses light rainfall and amplifies heavy precipitation; anthropogenic aerosols alone boost weak rainfall only.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes a mechanistic framework linking dust ice-nucleation to extreme precipitation globally; suggests dust aerosol loading must be accounted for in regional heavy-precipitation forecasting and attribution, especially where anthropogenic aerosols are rising.
- **Caveats:** Magnitude of the +9.6 mm signal is a global maximum — regional and seasonal variability unclear from abstract; causal direction (dust driving rain vs. synoptic conditions co-occurring with both) not fully resolved from observational analysis alone.
