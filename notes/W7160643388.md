# [Rainwater Regulation Alters Water Partitioning and Vegetation Growth on the Loess Plateau](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042657)

**Authors:** Shenghui Zhou, Baoqing Zhang, Xining Zhao et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042657
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Incorporating rainwater harvesting into Noah-MP shows ~52% of captured runoff goes to ET, boosting LAI 6% and WUE 3.7% yr⁻¹ across China's Loess Plateau.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Rainwater regulation (capturing surface runoff to recharge soil moisture) is widely practiced in dryland restoration, but its large-scale ecohydrological effects on land surface processes are poorly quantified within LSM frameworks.
- **Method:** Rainwater regulation module added to the Noah-MP land surface model; applied across China's Loess Plateau 2001–2018 using spatially explicit precipitation and soil data to partition captured surface runoff into ET, subsurface runoff, and storage.
- **Key result:** When surface runoff is fully utilized, ~52% is allocated to ET, 21% to subsurface runoff (Q_sub), and 27% to water storage. LAI and WUE increased annually by 6% and 3.7%, respectively. Soil moisture in the northwest Loess Plateau declined post-implementation, driven by soil hydraulic redistribution and enhanced plant uptake. Ecohydrological response showed cumulative effects over time.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that optimizing rainwater regulation intensity can improve vegetation outcomes even under stationary precipitation, informing sustainable restoration design on the Loess Plateau and analogous dryland regions without relying on increased rainfall.
- **Caveats:** Abstract does not report validation against observed soil moisture or streamflow; effects of groundwater dynamics and lateral redistribution are unclear; findings are specific to Loess Plateau soil and vegetation conditions.
