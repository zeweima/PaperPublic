# [How Will Higher Interannual Precipitation Variability Intensify Water Stress Under a Drying Climate?](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042767)

**Authors:** Hongxing Zheng, Francis Chiew, David Post et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042767
**Score:** 9/10 <sup>top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Higher precipitation variability under drying climate reduces runoff and water supply reliability in SE Australia, but mean rainfall decline dominates over variability effects.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Climate change alters both mean and variability of precipitation; the hydrological consequences of increased variability are less well understood than those from mean changes alone.
- **Method:** Sensitivity experiments and hydrological projections across 392 catchments in south-east Australia using three rainfall-runoff models and a concatenated behavior analysis of a virtual reservoir; climate signals drawn from global climate models targeting ~2060 vs. ~1990 conditions.
- **Key result:** Mean annual runoff projected to decline 18% in the southern part of the region and 6% in the north by ~2060; water supply reliability falls 13% (south) and 7% (north). Annual runoff variability increases but less than precipitation variability, buffered by catchment storage. Regulated systems show smaller reliability impacts due to reservoir buffering.
- **Why it matters:** Separates the variability-driven component of water stress from the dominant mean-drying signal; shows reservoir systems partially buffer variability but cannot offset large mean declines — relevant for water supply planning under climate change in semi-arid/Mediterranean regions.
- **Caveats:** Virtual reservoir analysis may not capture real operational rules; three models provide ensemble spread but structural uncertainty in rainfall-runoff representation remains; findings are regionally specific to SE Australia and may not generalise to other drying climates.
