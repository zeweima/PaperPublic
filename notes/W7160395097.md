# [Pollinators support the nutrition and income of vulnerable communities](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10421-x)

**Authors:** T. P. Timberlake, S. Sapkota, N. M. Saville et al.
**Venue:** Nature · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10421-x
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Insect pollinators account for 44% of farming income and >20% of vitamin A, folate, and vitamin E intake in Nepali smallholder communities.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Biodiversity loss degrades pollination services, but mechanistic links from pollinator decline to specific human nutrition and income outcomes remain unquantified at the individual level.
- **Method:** Individual-level diets, crop yields, farming income, and crop–pollinator interaction networks measured in replicate smallholder communities in Nepal; pollinator contributions to nutrient flows and household income calculated directly.
- **Key result:** Pollinators responsible for 44% of farming income, >20% of vitamin A, folate, and vitamin E intake; declines in local pollinator species projected to increase poverty and micronutrient deficiency rates; native honeybees, bumblebees, and hoverflies identified as most important taxa.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a quantitative, mechanistic framework linking local biodiversity to human health outcomes; shows that managing local pollination services is an actionable lever for nutrition and income improvement in smallholder systems.
- **Caveats:** Single region (Nepal); results may not transfer to other crop systems, pollinator communities, or socioeconomic contexts; long-term dynamics of pollinator management effects not addressed.
