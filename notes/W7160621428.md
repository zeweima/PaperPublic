# [Understanding meteorological, runoff, and agricultural drought propagation and their influencing factors in an ensemble of multiple datasets](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2775-2026)

**Authors:** Y.R. Liu, Tingting Hu, Jiawen Yang et al.
**Venue:** HESS (Hydrology and Earth System Sciences) · **Date:** 2026-05-08 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2775-2026
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Global drought propagation from meteorological to agricultural takes ~8.7 months on average, with temperature/PET controlling runoff propagation and precipitation controlling agricultural drought propagation.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How meteorological drought propagates to runoff and agricultural droughts at global scale, and which climatic/geographic factors govern propagation speed, rate, and lag, is poorly constrained across diverse regions.
- **Method:** Multi-dataset ensemble (ERA5, GLDAS, TerraClimate) over global land, 1958–2024; standardized drought indices from precipitation, runoff, and soil moisture; two frameworks — time-lag correlation and multi-threshold run theory; SHAP-based attribution of climatic/geographic controls on response time (RT), propagation rate (PR), and lag time (LT).
- **Key result:** Meteorological-to-runoff drought: RT = 5.0 months, PR = 55.3%, LT = 1.23 months. Meteorological-to-agricultural drought: RT = 8.7 months, PR = 30.3%, LT = 2.60 months. Runoff-to-agricultural drought: RT = 5.8 months, PR = 35.0%, LT = 2.49 months. Temperature and PET are primary drivers of met→runoff propagation; precipitation dominates met→agricultural and runoff→agricultural propagation. Pronounced spatiotemporal heterogeneity linked to regional climate background.
- **Why it matters:** Quantitative global benchmarks for drought propagation timing and rates improve drought early-warning systems and regional risk management; SHAP attribution identifies which climate variables to monitor for each drought type transition.
- **Caveats:** Ensemble spread among ERA5, GLDAS, and TerraClimate not fully reported; standardized indices may smooth inter-dataset differences; results aggregated globally may obscure local non-stationarity over the 66-year period.
