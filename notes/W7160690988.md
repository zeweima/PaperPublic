## TL;DR

Skipping the Kendall distribution when normalizing multivariate drought indices biases drought occurrence estimates; proper standardization requires it.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Univariate drought indices use the Probability Integral Transform (PIT) to map to a Gaussian domain; extending this to multivariate settings is non-trivial, and common practice of applying the joint CDF directly without Kendall-distribution correction violates Gaussianity and biases results.
- **Method:** Theoretical derivation showing that correct multivariate Gaussian normalization requires the Kendall distribution function (not the raw joint CDF); supported by both analytical proof and empirical illustrations.
- **Key result:** Omitting the Kendall distribution correction leads to biased estimation of drought occurrence frequencies; magnitude of bias is demonstrated through theoretical and empirical analyses (quantitative extent not specified in abstract).
- **Why it matters:** Many multivariate drought index implementations (e.g., copula-based MSDI, multivariate SPI variants) implicitly apply incorrect standardization; results provide a rigorous correction that should be adopted in operational monitoring and frequency analysis.
- **Caveats:** Empirical case studies are illustrative rather than a comprehensive multi-basin validation; practical impact of bias on real-world drought management decisions is not quantified.
