# [The pace of meeting carbon emission targets alters regional climate risks](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec4566)

**Authors:** In‐Hong Park, Sang-Wook Yeh, Andrew D King
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec4566
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Reaching the same carbon budget faster produces stronger land warming and greater heat-wave exposure in 80–85% of land regions, driven by slower deep-ocean heat uptake.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Carbon budgets assume cumulative emissions govern warming, but whether the rate of reaching a target budget changes regional climate outcomes is unquantified.
- **Method:** Multi-model comparison across 29 CMIP-class climate models; paired scenarios converging to identical total carbon emissions via fast vs. slow emission trajectories; analysis of 46 land regions for temperature and heat-wave exposure.
- **Key result:** Faster emission trajectories produce greater land warming and higher heat-wave exposure in 37/46 regions (80%) and 39/46 regions (85%), respectively, relative to slower pathways reaching the same total budget.
- **Why it matters:** Mitigation policy currently focuses almost exclusively on cumulative emissions (carbon budget framing); these results show that temporal pacing of emissions is an independent determinant of regional climate risk, particularly for heat extremes.
- **Caveats:** Mechanism attributed to deep-ocean heat uptake lag but model spread across 29 models not detailed in abstract; results apply to land regions only; interaction with overshoot scenarios not discussed.
