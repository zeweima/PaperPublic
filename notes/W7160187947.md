# [Probabilistic Agro‐Hydrology: A Stochastic Framework for Irrigation Risk Assessment and Water Management](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041844)

**Authors:** D. D. Chiarelli, E. Volpi, A. Fiori, M.C. Rulli
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041844

## TL;DR

A stochastic agro-hydrological framework quantifies irrigation return periods across eight Italian regions, revealing steep frequency curves where small BW increases correspond to large return-period jumps.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Irrigation risk is assessed deterministically; no equivalent of flood-frequency analysis exists for extreme irrigation water demand, leaving infrastructure design and water management plans without probabilistic guidance.
- **Method:** Distributed agro-hydrological model driven by stochastically generated climate inputs (Monte Carlo ensembles); applied to 8 climatically diverse, high-productivity regions in Italy. Cross-correlation analysis quantifies interannual memory in blue water (BW) demand; return-period curves estimated for irrigation water needs.
- **Key result:** Near-zero interannual memory in BW demand found across all sites; return-period curves are steep — small increases in BW above the mean are associated with disproportionately large return periods. Northern Italy shows lower mean BW but higher interannual variability; Southern Italy shows higher but more stable BW tied to chronic scarcity and drought-adapted cropping.
- **Why it matters:** Reframes irrigation planning from deterministic simulation to probabilistic risk assessment analogous to flood design standards; enables estimation of irrigation infrastructure failure probabilities and supports climate-resilient agricultural water management.
- **Caveats:** Case study limited to Italy; transferability to regions with strong interannual teleconnections (e.g., monsoon-driven systems) untested. Stochastic climate generator assumptions may not capture non-stationary trends under climate change.
