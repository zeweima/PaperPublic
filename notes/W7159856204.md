# [Snow Water Storage Within Eight Pacific Coastal Watersheds in British Columbia (Canada) Inferred From Four Years of Airborne Lidar Data](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040837)

**Authors:** R. Bisset, W.C. Floyd, B. Menounos et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040837
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Four years of repeat airborne lidar over ~330 km² of coastal BC rainforest show peak snowpack holds 35–42% of annual surface runoff, concentrated 1,000–1,500 m a.s.l.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Maritime mountain snowpacks are poorly quantified globally; no multi-year, high-resolution snow water equivalent (SWE) benchmark exists for coastal rainforest watersheds, which differ fundamentally from continental snowpacks.
- **Method:** Airborne lidar surveys (3–5 per year, 2020–2023) of eight small-to-medium watersheds (5.2–63.4 km², total ~330 km²) in southwest BC; 14,747 manual snow depth and 1,912 density measurements used to train a random forest snow density model (RMSE = 33.6–73.8 kg m⁻³) for volume-to-mass conversion.
- **Key result:** 50–86% of peak seasonal snow mass stored between 1,000–1,500 m a.s.l.; peak snow mass = 35–42% of total annual surface runoff; pronounced watershed-to-watershed differences in altitudinal distribution driven by hypsometry and local climate.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes a benchmark dataset for validating regional/global SWE products in mountainous maritime regions; quantifies that future snowpack loss will significantly impair downstream water availability; reveals that warming impacts will be spatially heterogeneous across the region.
- **Caveats:** Only eight small-to-medium watersheds; four-year record may not capture interannual variability fully; density model RMSE up to ~74 kg m⁻³ introduces uncertainty in mass estimates; lidar surveys capture discrete snapshots rather than continuous time series.
