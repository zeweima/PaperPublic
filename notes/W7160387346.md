## TL;DR

Thermodynamic drivers of mid-latitude winter precipitation trends are model-observation consistent, but forced vs. unforced circulation changes remain unresolved.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Separating anthropogenically forced precipitation trends from internally driven natural variability in mid-latitude winter precipitation is poorly constrained.
- **Method:** Climate models combined with statistical learning to decompose thermodynamic (non-circulation) and dynamic (circulation-related) contributions to precipitation trends; assessed forced vs. unforced signals.
- **Key result:** Thermodynamic effects are largely consistent between models and observations; circulation-related precipitation changes cannot yet be attributed as forced or unforced.
- **Why it matters:** Clarifies which precipitation trend components are robustly attributable to human forcing vs. internal variability, guiding detection/attribution frameworks and climate projections.
- **Caveats:** Attribution of circulation-driven changes remains ambiguous; the scope is limited to mid-latitude winter precipitation; no quantitative partitioning fractions reported in abstract.
