# [Exploring forest structural controls on soil and litter evaporation via experimental simulations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111222)

**Authors:** Tyler E. McIntosh, C. Rhett Jackson, Caren C. Mendonca et al.
**Venue:** Agricultural and Forest Meteorology · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111222
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Leaf litter accumulation reduces forest-floor evaporation (51% at 800 g m⁻²) more strongly than canopy shade (32% at 91%), and their effects are additive but independent.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Soil and litter evaporation (E_sl) is a poorly constrained ET component; the relative mechanistic roles of canopy shading vs. forest floor litter in suppressing E_sl were unknown because both are inherently confounded in field settings.
- **Method:** Full-factorial lysimeter experiment (n = 7 non-weighing aluminum box lysimeters, 0.49 m² surface area) on well-drained sandy soil (Blanton sand) at Savannah River Site, SC. Four shade levels (0, 40, 71, 91% Rn reduction via shade cloth) × four litter levels (0, 200, 400, 800 g m⁻² dried loblolly pine needles); four replicates per treatment across ~21-day collection intervals. E_sl relativized to a bare-soil reference (E_Ref) strongly coupled to ETo, VPD, and Rn (r² = 0.93, 0.96, 0.83). Treatment-specific crop coefficients (K_e = slope of E_sl vs. ETo) quantified atmospheric coupling under each structural scenario.
- **Key result:** Shade and litter effects were independent (p = 0.672). Averaging across litter levels: 40, 71, 91% shade reduced E_Rel by 17, 27, 32%. Averaging across shade levels: 200, 400, 800 g m⁻² litter reduced E_Rel by 21, 41, 52%. At maximum levels without the other factor: 91% shade alone reduced E_Rel 27%; 800 g m⁻² litter alone reduced it 51%; combined reduced it 65%. Mean K_e change from 0→800 g litter m⁻² (ΔK_e = 0.273) was more than twice the change from 0→91% shade (ΔK_e = 0.128). E_sl remained positively coupled to ETo under all treatments — forest structure mutes, not decouples, atmospheric control.
- **Why it matters:** Litter removal (pine straw raking, clear-cut harvesting) substantially raises E_sl and K_e, while canopy thinning elicits subtler responses; maintaining forest floor litter is therefore a more effective management lever for minimizing non-productive water loss than managing canopy cover alone. K_e coefficients across the shade × litter factorial provide a framework for parameterizing forest-floor evaporation in ET models as stand structure evolves.
- **Caveats:** Experiment maintained energy-limited (non-water-limited) conditions on sandy soil; K_e values are upper bounds and will overestimate E_sl in water-limited or fine-textured systems. Shade cloth reduced Rn only, not the full canopy microclimate (temperature, VPD, turbulence). Litter was uniform dried pine needles, not heterogeneous or decomposing. Soil moisture measured only at 10 and 40 cm. Results most robust for relative treatment comparisons within this system; cross-forest-type generalization requires further work.
