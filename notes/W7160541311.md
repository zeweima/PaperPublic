# [Divergent biogeochemical responses in upland coastal forest soils to repeated flooding and shifts in water chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10533-026-01340-0)

**Authors:** Rosmery Cruz-O'Byrne, Ashleigh Montgomery, Angelia Seyfferth et al.
**Venue:** Biogeochemistry · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10533-026-01340-0
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Brackish-water flooding of upland coastal forest soils drives stronger legacy reducing conditions, elevated CH4 and N2O, and organo-mineral carbon destabilization compared to freshwater pulses.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Sea-level rise and storm surges repeatedly flood upland coastal forests, but how floodwater salinity/chemistry shapes early-stage belowground biogeochemical transitions is poorly constrained.
- **Method:** Mesocosm flow-through incubation with intact soil monoliths (22 cm depth × 22 cm diameter) from a temperate upland coastal forest; alternating freshwater (FW) and brackish water (BW) pulse treatments; continuous CO2, CH4, N2O flux measurements paired with δ¹³C-CH4, porewater chemistry (DOC, DIC, SO4²⁻, HS⁻, Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺, NH4⁺, NO3⁻+NO2⁻, Eh, pH), and FTIR of soil organic functional groups.
- **Key result:** BW treatments produced stronger reducing conditions with higher CH4 and N2O fluxes and larger legacy effects than FW treatments. Elevated HS⁻ and δ¹³C-CH4 signatures indicated co-occurring sulfate reduction and methylotrophic methanogenesis. NH4⁺ accumulation and N2O pointed to DNRA and incomplete denitrification. Rising Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺, and DOC indicated organo-mineral association breakdown; FTIR showed polysaccharide degradation and incorporation of S- and N-containing functional groups.
- **Why it matters:** Brackish intrusion—not just flooding per se—triggers chemistry-dependent feedbacks that destabilize soil carbon stocks and amplify GHG emissions, accelerating ecosystem state change in ghost forests; these mechanisms need representation in coastal biogeochemical models.
- **Caveats:** Mesocosm scale (single site, 22 cm monoliths) limits spatial generalization; controlled pulses may not replicate natural flooding durations or mixing dynamics; long-term field validation needed.
