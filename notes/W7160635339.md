## TL;DR

In long-term tilled soils, microbial-driven carbon protection dominates organic carbon sequestration over active mineral-phase associations.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Relative importance of microbial pathways vs. mineral (reactive Fe/Al) protection for SOC stabilization in tilled agricultural soils is poorly resolved.
- **Method:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Key result:** Microbial protection mechanisms outweigh active mineral protection as the primary driver of organic carbon sequestration in long-term tilled soils.
- **Why it matters:** Challenges models that prioritize mineral-associated organic matter (MAOM) formation; implies that managing microbial community and necromass pathways may be more effective for building SOC than targeting reactive mineral surfaces under tillage regimes.
- **Caveats:** Abstract unavailable; scope (soil types, tillage systems, duration) and quantitative effect sizes unclear from title alone.
