# [Vertical conveyor driving the integration of moisture transported by the westerlies to the Asian water towers' atmospheric water cycle](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2529749123)

**Authors:** JS Gao, Tandong Yao, Valérie Masson-Delmotte et al.
**Venue:** PNAS · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2529749123
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Nighttime westerlies subsidence creates thermal inversions over the Asian water towers that decouple free-troposphere moisture from the boundary layer, accumulating near-surface moisture without precipitation.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Mechanisms by which large-scale westerlies-advected moisture enters the atmospheric water budget of the Asian water towers (AWTs) — which supply water to >2 billion people — are poorly constrained due to observational gaps.
- **Method:** 3-D observations of atmospheric water vapor stable isotopes combined with isotope-enabled modeling over the AWTs during winter-spring westerlies-dominant season.
- **Key result:** Sharp vertical isotopic gradients reveal westerlies moisture is confined aloft while local residual moisture persists near the surface; nighttime subsidence of westerlies air interacts with thermodynamically distinct local air, generating thermal inversions that suppress vertical mixing and allow westerlies moisture to integrate into the near-surface budget via condensation rather than precipitation.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies a previously uncharacterized "vertical conveyor" pathway for moisture delivery to the AWTs; provides benchmarks for improving atmospheric models and climate projections of the intensifying Asian water cycle, and refines interpretation of isotopic paleoclimate archives.
- **Caveats:** Observational coverage of the AWTs remains sparse; findings focus on calm winter-spring conditions and may not generalize to monsoon-dominated or stormy periods. Model isotope representation uncertain.
