# [Invariable hydraulics and rapidly tapering cross-sectional areas of lateral roots reduce water transport capacity to half at the crown periphery of mature Scots pine](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111187)

**Authors:** Donggyu Kam, Teemu Hölttä, Annikki Mäkelä et al.
**Venue:** Agricultural and Forest Meteorology · **Date:** 2026-05-02 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111187
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>

## TL;DR

Lateral root cross-sectional area tapering — not hydraulic conductivity variation — halves water transport capacity at the crown periphery of mature Scots pine.

## Summary
- **Problem:** How root architecture and hydraulic properties interact to constrain water delivery from soil to trunk in large trees, particularly at the radially distant crown edge.
- **Method:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Key result:** Water transport capacity is reduced to ~50% at the crown periphery relative to proximal root zones; invariable (uniform) hydraulic conductivity per unit area means rapid tapering of lateral root cross-sections is the dominant cause.
- **Why it matters:** Quantifying root-zone hydraulic bottlenecks is essential for accurate tree water-use models and for predicting drought vulnerability in boreal Scots pine stands.
- **Caveats:** Single species (Scots pine); whether findings generalize to other tree architectures or ages is unclear from available information.
