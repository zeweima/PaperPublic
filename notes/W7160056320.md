# [Modest ocean warming difference, explosive intensification: Marine heatwave control of Typhoon Hagibis (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6713)

**Authors:** Hwan-Young Choi, Myung‐Sook Park, Chaehyeon Chelsea Nam et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6713

**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Marine heatwave conditions drove Typhoon Hagibis to intensify 20–40% faster than non-MHW SST conditions, with even a 1 °C surface warming sufficient to trigger explosive rapid intensification.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Typhoon Hagibis (2019) intensified by 100 kt in 24 h — more than 3× the conventional rapid intensification (RI) threshold of ~30 kt/24 h — yet the specific role of marine heatwave (MHW) ocean structure in enabling such explosive RI (ERI) was unquantified.
- **Method:** Coupled WRF + 3D Price–Weller–Pinkel ocean model; control run (MHW conditions) compared against two sensitivity experiments substituting non-MHW SSTs and vertical temperature profiles from Typhoon Jebi (2018), a comparable RI case.
- **Key result:** Hagibis intensified ~20% faster under MHW baseline conditions; including realistic vertical ocean mixing raised that advantage to ~40% faster intensification relative to non-MHW profiles. Elevated subsurface heat content sustained air-sea enthalpy exchange despite storm-induced SST cooling; a 1 °C surface temperature increase was sufficient to modulate TC structure and amplify ERI.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the MHW threshold for ERI enhancement is lower than previously assumed; as MHW frequency and intensity rise, the current ERI definition (~30 kt/24 h) may need revision to capture the upper tail of intensification rates now possible under warmer upper-ocean conditions.
- **Caveats:** Single-case study (one typhoon, one basin); sensitivity experiments use SST/profile from a different storm rather than a paired observational baseline; model resolution and parameterization choices could affect the quantitative intensification differences.
