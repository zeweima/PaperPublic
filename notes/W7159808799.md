# [Soil pH Amelioration Fosters Persistent Carbon Sinks Through Mineral Stabilization and Aggregate Protection](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70896)

**Authors:** Xunzhuo Dong, Bingbing Han, Yunyao Zhong et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70896
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Soil pH amelioration increases bulk SOC 18–20% by routing carbon into mineral-stabilized and aggregate-protected pools, not labile fractions.

## Summary

- **Problem:** SOC stability is critical for agricultural carbon credit integrity, but the specific effect of pH amelioration on SOC components is confounded by organic matter inputs in most studies.
- **Method:** Bivariate linear mixed-effects models applied to a global synthesis of 180 field trials; decomposed effects on bulk SOC, mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC), microbial necromass carbon (MNC), particulate organic carbon (POC), and aggregate fractions.
- **Key result:** pH amelioration raised bulk SOC 18–20%, MAOC 11–15%, MNC 12–19%, and microaggregate-C by up to 44% in alkaline soils. Structural equation modelling identified microbial biomass and aggregate stability as hierarchical drivers. POC and MAOC increments attenuated with time; MNC increment persisted. Manure substitution amplified all components; straw substitution showed weaker POC response than biochar or manure.
- **Why it matters:** Repositions precision pH management as an ecological engineering tool that shifts carbon flow toward stable pools; provides mechanistic basis for moving carbon credit protocols from stock-based to stability-centric verification.
- **Caveats:** Based on field trial synthesis (180 sites); causal inference limited by observational design; temporal attenuation of MAOC/POC effects needs longer-term studies; findings may be soil-type or climate-zone specific.
