# [A First Look at the Snow/Ice Penetration Effect of SWOT Observations on Water Level of Global Glacial Lakes](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122624)

**Authors:** Shuangxiao Luo, Chunqiao Song, Pengfei Zhan et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122624
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

SWOT Ka-band radar penetrates glacial lake snow/ice by −0.25 m on annual average (up to −1 m in winter), requiring correction before merging with ICESat-2 records.

## Summary

- **Problem:** SWOT's Ka-band radar penetrates snow/ice cover on glacial lakes, causing an elevation bias relative to the true surface — magnitude unquantified until now, undermining water-level change assessments.
- **Method:** Intercomparison of near-synchronous SWOT and ICESat-2 surface elevations over 260 glacial lakes (>1 km²) globally across a 1-year period; ICESat-2 laser treated as snow/ice surface reference.
- **Key result:** Globally consistent negative bias of −0.25 m (SWOT minus ICESat-2); bias more than doubles seasonally from −0.15 m (warm season) to −0.34 m (cold season), with peak monthly values near −1 m.
- **Why it matters:** Provides empirical correction framework to reconcile SWOT and ICESat-2, enabling long-term, high-resolution glacial lake level records that are otherwise contaminated by this systematic bias.
- **Caveats:** Results are based on one year of SWOT data; penetration depth likely varies with ice/snow physical properties (density, grain size, liquid water content) not fully characterized here.
