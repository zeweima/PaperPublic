# [Glacier Melt Enhances Suspended Sediment and Mercury Export from the Tibetan Plateau](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c01369)

**Authors:** Mingyue Li, Dongfeng Li, Xuejun Sun et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c01369
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Tibetan Plateau glacier-fed streams export total Hg at yields rivaling Alaska, driven by erosion-linked particle-associated Hg that intensifies with melt and rainfall.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Controls on suspended sediment (SS) and mercury (Hg) export from glacier-fed streams (GFSs) on the Tibetan Plateau are poorly constrained, limiting projections of downstream water quality under accelerating glacier retreat.
- **Method:** Field sampling (n ≈ 350) of SS and Hg concentrations across six Tibetan Plateau GFSs, synthesized with observations from 36 GFSs worldwide; analysis by glacier thermal regime, coverage, velocity, and proglacial lake presence.
- **Key result:** Tibetan Plateau GFSs show total Hg (THg) concentrations and yields comparable to Alaskan GFSs but higher than most GFSs with similar glacier coverage. In GFSs without proglacial lakes, THg export scales with SS and increases with glacier coverage and velocity. THg–SS hysteresis patterns differ among thermal regimes, reflecting particle sorting and subglacial drainage evolution.
- **Why it matters:** Intensifying melt and rainfall are projected to increase Hg yields and delay peak Hg export timing; expanding proglacial lakes may temporarily buffer downstream export. Findings quantify glacier-retreat impacts on Hg loading to major Asian river systems.
- **Caveats:** Abstract text is partially truncated (begins mid-sentence with "= 350"), suggesting some context is missing. Limited to six Tibetan GFSs for primary data; global synthesis relies on heterogeneous third-party datasets. Proglacial lake buffering capacity and long-term trajectory are not quantified.
