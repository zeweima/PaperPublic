# [Subsurface soil inorganic carbon gains offset half of surface losses in China's upland croplands over the last four decades](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2531214123)

**Authors:** Andong Cai, Tianfu Han, Zhenghu Zhou et al.
**Venue:** PNAS · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2531214123
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Surface SIC losses in China's croplands are 44% offset by subsurface gains, but this reflects vertical redistribution, not net sequestration.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Soil inorganic carbon (SIC) dynamics across full soil profiles in croplands are poorly quantified; most studies focus on the surface horizon and assume SIC is stable on decadal timescales.
- **Method:** Paired-site resampling at 204 matched locations (4,305 soil profiles) across China's upland croplands, comparing 1980s legacy data against 2023 field measurements across a 1-m profile.
- **Key result:** Surface SIC (0–40 cm) declined by 0.68 kg m⁻², driven by increased precipitation and soil acidification; subsurface SIC (40–100 cm) increased by 0.49 kg m⁻² (0.48 Pg total), offsetting 44% of surface losses. Net profile-scale change is negative — redistribution, not sequestration.
- **Why it matters:** Depth-integrated SIC accounting changes the apparent carbon budget of China's croplands; ignoring subsurface gains overestimates net loss, while crediting them as sequestration overestimates climate mitigation. Both terrestrial carbon models and IPCC accounting need depth-resolved SIC dynamics.
- **Caveats:** China-specific; drivers (acidification, precipitation) may not generalize globally. Legacy site relocation introduces positional uncertainty. Mechanisms linking carbon inputs to subsurface pH increase need further investigation.
