# [Alternative vegetation states on the Loess Plateau and implications for large-scale afforestation success](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2527794123)

**Authors:** Li Ma, Xuan Li, Xingchao Xu et al.
**Venue:** PNAS · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2527794123
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Loess Plateau tree cover exhibits alternative stable states between 350–700 mm MAP, with afforestation success contingent on precipitation-driven bistability and tristability zones.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Billion-yuan afforestation programs on the Loess Plateau have boosted vegetation cover but depleted soil water; the role of alternative stable states — rather than a simple precipitation threshold — in determining long-term afforestation viability has been ignored.
- **Method:** Systematic field survey of 4,875 sites (80,000 km transects) measuring tree cover along a mean annual precipitation (MAP) gradient, combined with a minimal dynamical model incorporating vegetation-precipitation feedbacks.
- **Key result:** Three coexisting vegetation states (treeless <5%, open woodland 5–50%, forest >50%) between 350–500 mm MAP; bistability (open woodland vs. forest) between 500–700 mm MAP. Vegetation-precipitation positive feedbacks expand the bistable range and shift transition thresholds. Regime shifts across states carry large differences in carbon storage potential.
- **Why it matters:** Afforestation planning should target bistable and tristable precipitation zones where restoration to a higher-cover state is dynamically feasible; forcing tree cover in monostable treeless zones risks collapse and wasted investment.
- **Caveats:** Minimal model may not capture soil water depletion feedbacks explicitly; field survey is observational, so causality of feedback mechanisms is inferred; results specific to Loess Plateau dryland context.
