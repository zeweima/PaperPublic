# [Linking heavy rainfall to suspended sediment fluxes in a deglaciating Alpine catchment](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2717-2026)

**Authors:** Amalie Skålevåg, Lena Katharina Schmidt, Nele Eggers et al.
**Venue:** HESS (Hydrology and Earth System Sciences) · **Date:** 2026-05-08 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2717-2026
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

In deglaciating Austrian Alps, heavy precipitation events are increasing and now drive disproportionately high sediment fluxes, but cannot fully compensate for declining glacier-sourced sediment supply.

## Summary

- **Problem:** As Alpine glaciers retreat, the balance between glacier-driven and precipitation-driven suspended sediment supply shifts; the net effect on annual sediment yields is unclear.
- **Method:** 21-year hourly 1 km gridded precipitation dataset combined with suspended sediment concentration/yield records from two nested Austrian catchments (Tumpen-Ötztal and Vent-Rofental); multi-scale detection of heavy precipitation events classified by duration (sub-daily vs. long-duration) and spatial extent (localised vs. catchment-wide).
- **Key result:** Heavy precipitation event frequency and their fractional contribution to annual suspended sediment yield (SSY) both increased significantly over the study period. Sub-daily convective storms generate disproportionately high sediment fluxes. Despite this, annual SSY remains stable in Tumpen-Ötztal but declines in Vent-Rofental, indicating heavy-precipitation-driven transport partially offsets but does not replace glacier-driven supply.
- **Why it matters:** Alpine catchments are transitioning toward flashier sediment regimes; long-term sediment yields will likely decline as glacier retreat continues, with implications for downstream water quality, reservoir management, and geomorphic modeling.
- **Caveats:** Only two nested catchments in one mountain range; precipitation gridded data carry uncertainties explicitly noted by authors; the offsetting dynamic may not generalise to catchments with different glacier area fractions or lithologies.
