# [Temperature‐Driven Silicate Weathering Feedbacks Terminated the Middle Eocene Climatic Optimum](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121765)

**Authors:** Yiquan Ma, Majie Fan, Philip A.E. Pogge von Strandmann et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121765
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

High-resolution East Asian lake records show that temperature-driven silicate weathering — not rainfall decline — consumed enough CO₂ to end the Middle Eocene Climatic Optimum (~40 Ma).

## Summary

- **Problem:** The mechanism terminating the Middle Eocene Climatic Optimum (MECO, ~40 Ma) is unclear; continental hydroclimate responses during this event are poorly constrained.
- **Method:** High-resolution multi-proxy records (precipitation, chemical weathering, terrestrial input proxies) from four East Asian lake basin sediments; orbital forcing analysis identifying short-eccentricity, precession, and half-precession cycles.
- **Key result:** Before and during early MECO, intensified precipitation drove increased chemical weathering and physical erosion; at peak MECO, chemical weathering peaked despite reduced rainfall, indicating a shift to temperature-controlled weathering. Prior erosion had exposed large fresh silicate surfaces, enabling temperature-driven CO₂ drawdown that terminated the event.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that silicate weathering feedbacks can operate on orbital timescales via a two-stage mechanism (rainfall-primed exposure → temperature-driven drawdown), with implications for modeling CO₂ regulation in warm-climate analogs and deep-time carbon cycle models.
- **Caveats:** Based on four lake basins in East Asia — global representativeness unclear; quantitative CO₂ drawdown not provided; mechanism inferred from proxy co-variation rather than direct weathering flux measurements.
