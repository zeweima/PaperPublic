# [Zooplankton Metabolism Shapes Molecular Composition of Dissolved Organic Matter in Coastal Waters](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c05592)

**Authors:** Muhammad Firman Nuruddin, Yiu Cho Joe Poon, Xuejia He et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c05592
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Zooplankton metabolic gene expression — not just community composition — controls the molecular aromaticity and chemical makeup of DOM they release into coastal water.

## Summary

- **Problem:** DOM release by marine organisms is documented, but how organismal metabolic processes under different nutrient regimes shape the intrinsic molecular properties of released DOM is unknown.
- **Method:** Zooplankton DOM release experiments in estuarine-coastal systems across eutrophic vs. mesotrophic conditions; DOM characterized by FT-ICR-MS; zooplankton metabolic activity profiled via metatranscriptomics (community-level gene expression).
- **Key result:** DOM molecular composition differed substantially between eutrophic and mesotrophic communities; mesotrophic zooplankton expressed higher metabolic gene activity; DOM aromaticity indices strongly correlated with genes for carbohydrate, amino acid, lipid, terpenoid-polyketide, and glycan metabolism.
- **Why it matters:** Links organism-level metabolic state to DOM chemical quality, suggesting nutrient status of coastal waters mediates DOM aromaticity and reactivity — relevant to microbial loop efficiency and carbon export.
- **Caveats:** Lab-based incubation experiments; generalizability to open-ocean or highly dynamic estuaries unclear; metatranscriptomics captures expression at a snapshot, not steady-state fluxes.
