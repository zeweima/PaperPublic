# [Intercomparison of Methods for Disentangling Local and Nonlocal Biophysical Impacts of Land Cover Changes](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ef007704)

**Authors:** Zijun Zhao, Yan Li, Ru Xu et al.
**Venue:** Earth's Future · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ef007704
**Score:** 8/10 <sup>top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Systematic deforestation experiments show existing methods for separating local vs. nonlocal land-cover-change climate signals agree on latitudinal patterns but diverge regionally, with nonlocal effects dominating total temperature response.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Climate models simulate only total biophysical effects of land cover change (LCC) on temperature; several post-hoc methods exist to decompose local (within the LCC zone) and nonlocal (atmospheric teleconnection) effects, but they have never been benchmarked against each other under identical conditions.
- **Method:** Series of unified global deforestation experiments in a single climate model; multiple existing decomposition methods applied to the same outputs and compared against observational datasets for local surface temperature signals.
- **Key result:** Local effects show broadly consistent latitudinal and seasonal patterns across all methods, including the NH warming-to-cooling transition zone. Nonlocal effects dominate total LCC climate impact and are reproduced with broad similarity, but regional discrepancies arise from differing method assumptions.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a methodological guidance framework for choosing among decomposition approaches; differences between methods reveal structural assumptions that can bias attribution of LCC climate feedbacks at regional scales, relevant for LSM evaluation and land-use policy.
- **Caveats:** Based on deforestation experiments only; results may not generalize to other LCC types (afforestation, cropland expansion). Single model framework limits assessment of model-structural uncertainty.
