# [Organic amendments induce crop-specific soil biotic homogenization associated with SOC sequestration: Evidence from a 35-year intercropping experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107255)

**Authors:** Guizong Zhang, Jiudong Zhang, Rui-Peng Yu et al.
**Venue:** Soil and Tillage Research · **Date:** 2026-05-09 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107255
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

After 35 years of intercropping, organic amendments drive crop-specific convergence of soil biotic communities, and this homogenization is linked to SOC gains.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Whether long-term organic amendments alter soil biological communities differently under intercropped versus monocropped species, and how such shifts relate to SOC sequestration, is poorly resolved.
- **Method:** 35-year field intercropping experiment; treatments compared organic amendments vs. controls across crop species combinations; soil biotic community composition (likely microbiome/fauna) and SOC measured across treatments.
- **Key result:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the SOC sequestration benefit of organic amendments is mechanistically tied to biotic homogenization, and that this effect is crop-species-specific — relevant for designing amendment strategies in intercropping systems.
- **Caveats:** Single long-term site limits spatial generalizability; "biotic homogenization" metric and taxa groups examined not determinable from available metadata.
