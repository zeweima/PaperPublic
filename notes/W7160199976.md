## TL;DR

Free-living N fixation temperature sensitivity is higher in colder, high-elevation zones, so warming disproportionately amplifies N inputs where nutrients are already limiting.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Free-living biological N fixation (BNF) varies along elevation gradients, but whether soil properties or diazotrophic community structure drives those patterns — and how temperature sensitivity shifts with elevation — is poorly constrained.
- **Method:** Elevational transect study measuring free-living BNF rates and temperature sensitivity; paired soil resource characterization and diazotrophic community profiling (details not recoverable from truncated abstract).
- **Key result:** Soil and microbial (diazotrophic community structure and thermal traits) controls jointly regulate elevational BNF patterns; temperature sensitivity of BNF is higher in colder high-elevation zones, contingent on soil resource availability.
- **Why it matters:** Warming will likely cause greater increases in biological N inputs at high-elevation and high-latitude ecosystems — precisely where nutrient limitation is strongest; diazotrophic thermal traits need to be embedded in global biogeochemical models to improve N budget projections.
