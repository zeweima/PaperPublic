# [Exotic Plant Invasion Enhances Nitrous Oxide Emission and Methane Uptake in Forests and Grasslands: A Global Meta‐Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ef007893)

**Authors:** Tiehu He, Bahilu Bezabih Beyene, Lechisa Takele et al.
**Venue:** Earth's Future · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ef007893
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Exotic plant invasion raises net global warming potential by 59.7 ± 21.1 Tg CO₂ eq yr⁻¹ from forests and grasslands, driven by N₂O increases that outweigh CH₄ uptake gains.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How exotic plant invasions alter N₂O and CH₄ fluxes across biomes at global scale is poorly constrained, limiting invasion impacts in GHG budgets.
- **Method:** Global meta-analysis of 141 paired in situ observations across forests and grasslands; coupled with a global invasion-area dataset to scale flux changes from regional to global.
- **Key result:** Invasion raised temperate grassland N₂O from 1.35 ± 0.77 to 4.01 ± 0.97 kg N₂O ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (+197%); CH₄ uptake in tropical/subtropical forests increased from 2.56 ± 0.66 to 3.32 ± 0.63 kg CH₄ ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (+30%). Globally, invasion added 293.9 ± 68.2 Gg N₂O yr⁻¹ and increased CH₄ uptake by 762.1 ± 91.2 Gg CH₄ yr⁻¹, yielding a net positive warming forcing of 59.7 ± 21.1 Tg CO₂ eq yr⁻¹; temperate regions drove ~60% of N₂O increase and ~68% of CH₄ uptake gain.
- **Why it matters:** Invasion-driven N₂O enhancement is a largely unaccounted GHG source; under future climate change facilitating invasion spread, this warming forcing will grow and should be incorporated into terrestrial GHG accounting.
- **Caveats:** Sample sizes are limited in some biomes; forest N₂O showed no significant response, contrasting with grasslands; mechanism attribution (N cycling, soil moisture changes) is inferred rather than directly tested.
