# [Unveiling Redox-Active Quinones in Pyrogenic Carbon by Nontargeted Metabolomics and Dual Chemical Tagging](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c01199)

**Authors:** Anil Timilsina, Srinidhi Lokesh, Abrar Shahriar et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-08 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c01199
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Dual chemical tagging + high-resolution MS identifies previously unknown quinones in pyrogenic carbon contributing 2.3–11.5% of its redox capacity.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Quinones in natural organic matter drive methane oxidation, iron reduction, and contaminant degradation, but their molecular identities in compositionally complex matrices (pyrogenic carbon, NOM) are largely unknown.
- **Method:** Dual chemical tagging workflow combined with high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry applied to a pyrogenic carbon sample; meta-analysis of >40,000 spectra from public spectral repositories spanning 33–819 environmental samples across temperate to polar ecosystems.
- **Key result:** Identified previously undisclosed benzoquinones, alkylated chromene-diones, and hydroxylated naphthoquinones; estimated reduction-oxidation potential 0.21–0.41 V (pH 7); quinones contribute 2.3–11.5% of redox capacity in natural and pyrogenic organic matter.
- **Why it matters:** Provides molecular-level resolution of quinone pools that control electron shuttling in soils and sediments; enables more accurate modeling of quinone-mediated biogeochemical and contaminant transformation dynamics.
- **Caveats:** Focused on one representative pyrogenic sample; redox potential estimates are computed rather than directly measured; environmental relevance across diverse OM types needs further validation.
