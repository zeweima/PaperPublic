# [A comparative study of methods to quantify wheat yield penalty attributable to soil sodicity across north-eastern Australia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107209)

**Authors:** Yunru Lai, Matthew J. Pringle, Peter M. Kopittke et al.
**Venue:** Soil & Tillage Research · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107209
**Score:** n/a/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Three methods for mapping sodicity-induced wheat yield penalties across Australia's Northern Grains Region produce systematically divergent estimates — APSIM largest, yield-monitor empirical smallest regionally — driven by differences in data support and process representation rather than disagreement in system behaviour.

## Summary

- **Problem:** No consistent framework exists for quantifying soil-sodicity yield losses at resolution relevant to growers across Australia's Northern Grains Region (NGR), where 65–88% of wheat land is sodic; prior national estimates (AUD$1300M yr⁻¹) lack farm-scale applicability.
- **Method:** Three approaches compared over 2000–2016 at ~90 m (GlobalSoilMap) resolution: (1) Empirical Approach 1 — Cubist + linear mixed-effects model trained on 676 NSSC soil profiles with Landsat iNDVI as yield proxy, penalties kriged regionwide; (2) Empirical Approach 2 — same model framework trained on yield-monitor data from 60 maps across 17 farms, predictions applied via mapped ESP rasters; (3) APSIM process-based simulations at soil-profile locations using a kl-factor sodicity modifier (ESP threshold 15%), cultivar-matched per site, penalties spatially interpolated. Climate scenarios (wet/moderate/dry) defined by August vapour pressure deficit percentiles.
- **Key result:** Regional long-term mean yield penalties: Empirical Approach 1 = 0.213 Mg ha⁻¹, Empirical Approach 2 = 0.078 Mg ha⁻¹, APSIM = 0.259 Mg ha⁻¹. APSIM penalties peak under wet conditions (0.328 Mg ha⁻¹ mean) because higher yield potential amplifies the absolute deficit; Empirical Approach 2 peaks under dry conditions (0.198 Mg ha⁻¹ mean) because high VPD intensifies hydraulic limitation. Spatially, APSIM classifies 97–99% of the NGR as exceeding 0.1 Mg ha⁻¹ penalty vs. 89–91% for Empirical Approach 1 and 8–39% for Empirical Approach 2. At Farm 1 case study, all approaches converge on mean penalties of ~0.44–0.85 Mg ha⁻¹, roughly 2–4× the regional average.
- **Why it matters:** The three approaches are complementary across scales: Empirical Approach 1 is best for stable regional attribution; Empirical Approach 2 for within-field diagnosis where yield monitors exist; APSIM for process-based counterfactual scenario analysis and estimating potential gain from remediation. Together they provide a defensible multi-scale framework for prioritising sodicity management in dryland cropping systems.
- **Caveats:** APSIM kl modifier was calibrated only for Vertosol subsoil sodicity (ESP threshold 15 vs. 6 used empirically), missing topsoil effects and waterlogging; iNDVI yield proxy attenuates spatial variability in Empirical Approach 1; Empirical Approach 2 limited to spatially clustered farm data, poor regional extrapolation; ESP alone does not capture dispersive behaviour governed by electrolyte concentration; co-occurring constraints (salinity, Cl toxicity, acidity) not explicitly separated; August VPD is a coarse seasonal proxy.
