# [Mechanistic Insights into PFAS Rejection in Nanofiltration and Reverse Osmosis from Data-Driven Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c02287)

**Authors:** Yukai Tomsovic, Siwei Gu, Kyle Doudrick et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c02287

## TL;DR

ML analysis of 2353 literature data points shows membrane water permeance and PFAS molecular volume dominate rejection, while DOM and ion effects are nonmonotonic and condition-dependent.

## Summary
- **Problem:** PFAS structural diversity and inconsistent published results across studies prevent a unified understanding of which membrane/solution/solute properties control PFAS rejection in nanofiltration (NF) and reverse osmosis (RO).
- **Method:** Compiled 2353 rejection data points from the literature spanning diverse PFAS, membranes, feedwater compositions, and operating conditions; modeled the dataset with machine learning evaluating 13 experimental descriptors.
- **Key result:** Membrane water permeance and PFAS molecular volume had the strongest main effects, confirming steric exclusion as the primary mechanism. Low dissolved organic matter (DOM) and ion concentrations enhanced rejection (complexation increases apparent solute size); high concentrations reduced rejection (charge shielding and concentration polarization dominate).
- **Why it matters:** Resolves previously contradictory literature by attributing discrepancies to competing, concentration-dependent mechanisms; provides a framework to guide targeted membrane design and PFAS treatment strategy selection.
- **Caveats:** Data compiled from heterogeneous literature sources with varying experimental protocols; ML framework is correlative and may not fully capture membrane-specific transport physics; critical data gaps identified but not yet filled.
