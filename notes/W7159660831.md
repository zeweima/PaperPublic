# [Impact of soil heterogeneity and lateral heat fluxes on soil temperature simulations in a permafrost-affected soil](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-3509-2026)

**Authors:** Melanie A. Thurner, Xavier Rodriguez-Lloveras, Christian Beer
**Venue:** Geoscientific Model Development · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-3509-2026
**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Ignoring centimeter-scale soil heterogeneity in permafrost LSMs causes a systematic cold bias of ~0.2 °C annually, deepens active layers by up to 20 cm, and extends snow-free season by ~5 days — driven by nonlinear effects of organic matter on thermal properties.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Land-surface models aggregate soil properties over km-scale grids, erasing centimeter-scale heterogeneity in permafrost soils (cryoturbation-redistributed organic matter). The magnitude of this aggregation error and the role of lateral heat fluxes in amplifying it are unknown.
- **Method:** New 2D pedon-scale model DynSoM-2D (10 cm × 10 cm resolution, 50 m depth, 1-hour timestep) applied at a non-sorted circle site near Cherskii, East Siberia (68.7°N). Three setups compared: M0 — homogeneous (LSM-like averaged) soil; M1 — heterogeneous soil, no lateral heat flux; M2 — heterogeneous soil with lateral heat conduction. Forced with 14-year CRUNCEP v7 data; 50-year spinup.
- **Key result:** Heterogeneous soil simulates warmer annual mean temperatures than the homogeneous reference (+0.19 °C M1 vs. M0; +0.25 °C M2 vs. M0). Daily grid-cell biases reach up to 5 °C in summer topsoil. Active layer deepens 10–20 cm in heterogeneous runs; snow-free period extends up to 5 days. Annual soil-atmosphere heat budget differences are <1 W m⁻² across all setups (M0: 13.7, M1: 14.1, M2: 13.5 W m⁻²). Lateral fluxes reduce intra-model spatial spread but increase the domain-mean warming and make it spatially consistent. The aggregation error traces to nonlinear dependence of thermal conductivity and heat capacity on organic matter content — OM-rich patches lower mean κ more than an even OM distribution would.
- **Why it matters:** Even a 0.2 °C mean warm bias from neglected heterogeneity alters active layer depth and growing season length by >1 week when snow-melt and thaw effects combine — enough to affect decomposition rates, plant rooting depth, and C-balance simulations. Tiling schemes cannot fully correct this because they cannot represent the actual spatial distribution of OM within a grid cell. Results suggest standard LSMs systematically underestimate permafrost soil temperatures, with downstream consequences for C-cycle projections.
- **Caveats:** Single East Siberian site; direction of aggregation error could differ at drier sites (model runs near saturation, which favors OM-warming signal). Lateral water fluxes and heat advection not included — could amplify or dampen results. MOST surface-flux closure not strictly valid at decimeter patch scale. 3D effects ignored.
