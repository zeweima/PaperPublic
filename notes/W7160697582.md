# [Informing Robust Functional Relationship Benchmarks: An Evaluation of the Temperature Sensitivity of Ecosystem Respiration Across the Arctic‐Boreal Region](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009307)

**Authors:** Jeralyn Poe, Deborah Huntzinger, W. J. Riley et al.
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009307
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Benchmark construction choices — number of daily observations and temporal extent — drive larger differences in inferred land model skill than the choice of observational dataset itself.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Functional relationship benchmarks (e.g., apparent Q₁₀ of ecosystem respiration vs. temperature) are widely used to evaluate land surface models, but how benchmark construction choices affect inferred model skill is poorly understood, risking misattribution of model performance.
- **Method:** Systematic evaluation across Alaska and Canada (2001–2020, 20 years) testing the effects of dataset choice, number of daily observations, temporal extent, and temporal resolution on observed Q₁₀ values; applied to 13 models from the Warming Permafrost Model Intercomparison Project (WrPMIP).
- **Key result:** Inferred model skill varies substantially across benchmark configurations; sensitivity is greatest to number of daily observations and temporal extent, followed by dataset choice and temporal averaging. Observed Q₁₀ is highly variable both spatially and as a function of processing approach.
- **Why it matters:** Guidelines developed here provide a reproducible framework for constructing consistent functional relationship benchmarks, reducing spurious variation in model evaluation for permafrost and Arctic-boreal carbon cycle studies.
- **Caveats:** Analysis restricted to ecosystem respiration Q₁₀; generalizability to other functional relationships (e.g., GPP–light, CH₄–temperature) not demonstrated; spatial representativeness depends on available flux tower coverage across the region.
