# [Downscaling Daily Discharge to Sub‐Daily Scales for Alpine Glacierized Catchments](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040699)

**Authors:** A.-L. Argentin, M. Gianini, B. Schaefli et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr040699
**Score:** 9/10 <sup>top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

A maximum-entropy (POME) framework calibrated on 45 years of 15-min records from seven Swiss glacier catchments accurately downscales daily to sub-daily discharge, with a sigmoid function capturing seasonal diel cycle dynamics.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Sub-daily streamflow dynamics in glacierized alpine catchments — driven by temperature-controlled snow/ice melt and seasonally evolving subglacial drainage — cannot be recovered from daily model outputs due to coarse meteorological inputs and poor meltwater-routing parameterizations.
- **Method:** Maximum entropy (Principle of Maximum Entropy, POME) framework adapted for glacial systems; calibrated against a 45-year, 15-min discharge dataset from seven glacier-fed catchments in the southwestern Swiss Alps; forced with daily outputs (discharge, snow depth, ice melt) from a semi-lumped hydrological model.
- **Key result:** A sigmoid function effectively represents seasonally varying daily flow duration curves; the method reconstructs sub-daily discharge well against observations and reveals a detectable climate-warming signal in sub-daily flow dynamics over recent decades.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a data-parsimonious tool for reconstructing sub-daily discharge in data-sparse glacierized catchments, enabling finer-scale hydrological modeling for flood risk, hydropower, and ecological assessments under glacier retreat.
- **Caveats:** Calibrated exclusively in the southwestern Swiss Alps; transferability to other glacial regimes (e.g., polythermal or debris-covered glaciers) is untested. Performance depends on the semi-lumped model's ability to partition snow and ice melt accurately.
