# [A 481-meter-high landslide-tsunami in a cruise ship–frequented Alaska fjord](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec3187)

**Authors:** Dan H. Shugar, Katherine R. Barnhart, Mira Berdahl et al.
**Venue:** Science · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec3187
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

A massive 481 m tsunami in an Alaska fjord was preceded by days of microseismicity and generated a 36-hour seiche signal detectable globally.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Landslide-tsunami hazards in glacially conditioned fjords are poorly characterized, yet these environments are increasingly visited by cruise ships and face growing instability under climate change.
- **Method:** Multi-source analysis including seismic monitoring (microseismicity, long-period seismic waves), seiche signal characterization, and event reconstruction in a cruise ship-frequented Alaska fjord.
- **Key result:** Tsunami run-up reached 481 m; landslide produced long-period seismic waves equivalent to M5.4 earthquake; a ~66 s seiche persisted for up to 36 hours — only the second documented case of a days-long seiche observed seismically; failure was preceded by microseismicity increasing in rate and magnitude for several days, escalating ~1 hour before collapse.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes that precursory microseismicity may offer a detectable warning window; the persistent seiche signal is a novel observation with implications for remote hazard monitoring; highlights underappreciated risk to cruise ship traffic in high-latitude fjords under accelerating glacier retreat.
- **Caveats:** Abstract only — magnitude of landslide volume, specific failure mechanism, and statistical significance of precursory seismicity patterns not assessable from available text.
