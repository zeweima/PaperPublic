## TL;DR

A new framework — "ecotech" — unifies ecology, engineering, and earth science to scale nature-based solutions for restoration, carbon storage, water quality, and disaster resilience.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Biotechnology operates from the organism inward (molecular to organ scale) but today's challenges — biodiversity loss, climate change, pollution — require technological approaches that operate from the organism outward, at population, community, ecosystem, and biosphere scales. No integrating framework exists.
- **Method:** Conceptual framework development drawing on 80 years of biotechnology precedent; proposes ecotech as a convergence accelerator integrating ecology, engineering, and earth science, and identifying emerging sub-fields (ecomimicry, ecosystem materials science, ecosystem sensing and signaling).
- **Key result:** Framework defines actionable domains — nature restoration, carbon storage, water quality improvement, ecosystem energy, infrastructure disaster resistance — guided by cross-cutting principles of scalability, equity, and accountability. <unclear from abstract> on quantitative benchmarks.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a shared language and institutional pathway (NGOs, business, governments) for coordinating disparate nature-based technologies; positions ecotech as a complement to biotechnology for addressing existential environmental risks.
- **Caveats:** Purely conceptual; no empirical tests of efficacy or scalability reported; success depends on cross-sector adoption and governance structures not yet in place.
