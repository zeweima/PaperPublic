# [Future Decline in the Buffering Effect of Canopy Water on Ecosystem Water‐Use Efficiency in Tropical Forests](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042151)

**Authors:** H. W. Wu, C. S. Fu, K. L. Yu et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042151
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Canopy water buffers tropical forest WUE decline under water stress today, but that buffer collapses 88% under future atmospheric aridity by 2086–2100.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Tropical forest ecosystem water-use efficiency (WUE) is declining under increasing atmospheric aridity and soil drought, yet the role of canopy water dynamics in moderating that decline is unknown.
- **Method:** Integrated satellite-derived, observational, and reanalysis datasets with CMIP6 projections and CLM historical (2001–2015) and future (2086–2100) simulations; developed a canopy water amount index and a two-stage Partial Least Squares Regression to partition canopy water contributions to WUE change.
- **Key result:** Canopy water buffered WUE decline by 18% under atmospheric aridity and 9% under soil drought during 2001–2015, primarily via stomatal conductance relief and enhanced N availability for photosynthesis. Under future escalating atmospheric aridity (2086–2100), this buffering function decreases by 88%.
- **Why it matters:** Loss of canopy water buffering signals heightened risk of plant hydraulic failure and destabilization of carbon–water feedbacks and tropical forest resilience under climate change.
- **Caveats:** CLM simulations may not fully capture isohydric behavior or canopy interception dynamics; projections rely on CMIP6 ensemble spread and single land model; mechanisms inferred from regression rather than manipulation experiments.
