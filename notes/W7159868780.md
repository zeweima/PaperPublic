# [Nitrogen Release From Permafrost Thaw May Partially Offset Future Soil Carbon Losses](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70856)

**Authors:** Rémi Gaillard, P Cadule, Philippe Peylin et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70856
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Permafrost thaw releases nitrogen that can fertilise vegetation and partially offset soil carbon losses, but the offset magnitude is highly uncertain and likely overestimated by current models.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Permafrost thaw is expected to release large soil carbon stocks to the atmosphere, but co-released nitrogen could stimulate plant uptake and dampen the net carbon-climate feedback — this nitrogen fertilisation effect has rarely been quantified.
- **Method:** Earth system / land surface modelling approach simulating coupled C–N dynamics under permafrost thaw scenarios; nitrogen availability feedbacks on vegetation productivity assessed across future projections.
- **Key result:** Increased nitrogen availability following thaw drives vegetation carbon uptake that partially offsets soil carbon losses; however, the magnitude of this offset is highly uncertain and likely overestimated by the model.
- **Why it matters:** If the nitrogen offset is weaker than modelled, the permafrost carbon-climate feedback is stronger than currently projected — mitigation strategies that assume partial self-correction via N fertilisation could be miscalibrated.
- **Caveats:** Vegetation carbon uptake response to nitrogen fertilisation post-thaw is a key uncertainty and may be overestimated; authors caution against underestimating the permafrost C-climate feedback in climate policy contexts.
