# [Aerosol and non-aerosol drivers of regional trends in top-of-atmosphere Albedo over 2002-2020](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae687e)

**Authors:** Noé Clément, Olivier Boucher, Johannes Quaas et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae687e
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Gradient Boosting decomposition of CERES/MODIS data shows cloud-fraction changes — at least half aerosol-driven — dominate TOA albedo trends across most regions 2002–2020.

## Summary

- **Problem:** TOA albedo trends reflect both aerosol forcing and non-aerosol feedbacks, but their relative contributions at regional scales remain poorly separated.
- **Method:** CERES and MODIS satellite retrievals 2002–2020 over Europe, Eastern North America, Northeastern Asia, and India; Gradient Boosting regression applied to monthly retrieval differences between high- and low-albedo/AOD periods to attribute albedo trends to aerosol optical depth, cloud fraction, liquid water path, cloud droplet number, surface albedo, and surface temperature.
- **Key result:** Albedo and aerosol optical depth declined 2002–2020 in Europe, Eastern North America, and Northeastern Asia, but both increased in India. Cloud-fraction changes are the primary albedo-trend driver in Europe, Northeastern Asia, and India, with ≥50% of that contribution linked to aerosol trends. In Eastern North America, aerosol-related droplet number and AOD changes account for most of the trend, with minimal cloud-fraction contribution. In Northeastern Asia, snow-cover-driven surface-albedo changes are also a major contributor.
- **Why it matters:** Quantified regional sensitivities of TOA albedo to each driver provide observational benchmarks for testing climate model albedo trends and constraining aerosol–cloud interaction magnitudes.
- **Caveats:** Analysis covers only 18 years, which may conflate forced trends with internal variability; Gradient Boosting attribution is statistical, not process-based; collinearity among aerosol/cloud variables is not fully resolved.
