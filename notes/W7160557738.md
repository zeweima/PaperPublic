# [Biomonitoring Reveals the Transition from Legacy to Emerging PFAS in Subtropical Estuaries: Dual Roles of Usage Shift and Environmental Parameters](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c00432)

**Authors:** Xinyan Wu, Guangyuan Lu, Wen‐Xiong Wang et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Science & Technology · **Date:** 2026-05-07 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c00432
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Oyster biomonitoring in the Pearl River Estuary shows total PFAS dropped 89% (48.4→5.56 ng/g dw) 2015–2020, but emerging substitutes now dominate the profile.

## Summary

- **Problem:** After regulatory restrictions on legacy PFAS, emerging alternatives were widely adopted, yet their spatiotemporal trajectories in estuarine biota and the environmental controls on their accumulation are largely unknown.
- **Method:** Oysters as passive biomonitors sampled across the Pearl River Estuary 2015–2020; spatiotemporal analysis of legacy and emerging PFAS concentrations alongside environmental covariates (pH, salinity, temperature) and land-use proxies.
- **Key result:** Total median PFAS concentration fell from 48.4 to 5.56 ng/g dry weight (−88.5%), driven by legacy compound declines; emerging PFAS (HFPO-DA, 6:2 FTS) remained elevated and shifted from minor constituents in 2015–2016 to dominant contributors by 2020. HFPO-DA spatial distribution correlated with industrialized/urbanized zones.
- **Why it matters:** Regulatory substitution has not reduced total PFAS exposure risk — it has shifted the compound profile to less-characterized emerging alternatives; environmental parameters (pH, salinity, temperature) modulate bioavailability, complicating risk assessment.
- **Caveats:** Single estuary system (Pearl River) limits geographic generalizability; oyster accumulation reflects bioavailable fraction, not total water-column load; abstract-only source, quantitative mechanistic detail on partitioning unclear.
