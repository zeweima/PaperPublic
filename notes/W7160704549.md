# [Case‐Selective Dynamical Downscaling Enables a Tenfold Cost Reduction for Extreme Precipitation Statistics](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122330)

**Authors:** Wout Dewettinck, Hans Van de Vyver, Daan Degrauwe et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-09 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122330
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Simulating only extreme-rainfall windows rather than continuous runs reproduces convection-permitting precipitation statistics at ~10% of full-simulation cost.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Convection-permitting regional climate simulations are computationally prohibitive for long-term large-ensemble runs over wide domains, limiting extreme precipitation statistics and storyline approaches.
- **Method:** Case-selective dynamical downscaling (CSDD): uses low-resolution precipitation as a predictor to identify time windows containing extreme events, then downscales only those windows to convection-permitting resolution; validated against a full 30-year continuous simulation.
- **Key result:** CSDD reproduces 1–6 hourly extreme precipitation statistics from the full continuous simulation at roughly 10% of the computational cost; cases run in parallel, further reducing wall time.
- **Why it matters:** Makes large-ensemble convection-permitting runs feasible for extreme-value statistics and climate storylines; bridges the gap between physically grounded dynamical downscaling and statistical approaches to climate extremes.
- **Caveats:** Relies on low-resolution precipitation as a predictor — may miss extremes not signaled at coarse resolution; performance for multi-day events or compound extremes not assessed; tested over one 30-year period and region.
