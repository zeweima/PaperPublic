# [Capturing Antarctic Precipitation With a 3D Atmospheric River Algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl120986)

**Authors:** K. Takahashi, J. Inoue, K. Sato et al.
**Venue:** Geophysical Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl120986
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

A new 3D atmospheric river detection algorithm shows ARs drive 30–60% of Antarctic annual precipitation despite occurring <10% of the time.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Conventional AR detection methods fail in Antarctica, leaving the contribution of ARs to Antarctic ice surface mass balance poorly quantified and inconsistent at global scale.
- **Method:** Extended a 2D AR detection scheme into a new three-dimensional AR algorithm applied over Antarctica; validated against field observations at Dome Fuji during the 44th Japanese Antarctic Research Expedition; applied climatologically over 1979–2023 using reanalysis data.
- **Key result:** At Dome Fuji, detected ARs were associated with >50% of significant precipitation events and ~40% of total precipitation. Climatologically (1979–2023), ARs occurred <10% of the time but contributed 30–60% of annual total precipitation; AR-related precipitation captured regional variability in long-term Antarctic precipitation trends.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes a consistent framework for quantifying AR influence on Antarctic mass balance globally; demonstrates that extreme episodic moisture transport events dominate accumulation, which is critical for ice sheet mass budget and sea-level projections.
- **Caveats:** Validation is limited to a single interior site (Dome Fuji); reanalysis-based climatology inherits known biases in polar moisture transport; algorithm performance at coastal vs. interior sites not fully compared.
