# [What can hydrological modelling gain from spatially explicit parameterization and multi-gauge calibration?](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2493-2026)

**Authors:** Xudong Zheng, Dengfeng Liu, Hao Wang et al.
**Venue:** HESS · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2493-2026
**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Spatially explicit VIC parameterization via MPR and multi-gauge calibration are mutually reinforcing: each alone hits a ceiling, combined they deliver consistent gains across all sub-basins.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Distributed hydrological models often use spatially uniform parameters calibrated to a single outlet gauge; the independent and joint effects of switching to spatially explicit parameterization and multi-gauge calibration remain poorly understood.
- **Method:** EF-SPM framework — VIC-5 + multiscale parameter regionalization (MPR) applied to the Upper Han River Basin (5 nested sub-basins, ~23 000 km²); 8 calibration cases crossing uniform/distributed parameterization × single/multi-gauge calibration objectives; CMA-ES (single-objective) and NSGA-II (multi-objective, KGE at 5 gauges); 2003–2018 daily streamflow; 40 trials × 20 individuals per case.
- **Key result:** Combined distributed parameterization + multi-gauge calibration (Case 5) ranks first or second in KGE across all sub-basins for both calibration (KGE up to 0.787) and validation (KGE up to 0.745). Spatially explicit parameterization alone improves high-flow bias by 18% (%BiasFHV₁ at Shiquan) but degrades low-flow performance by 8.6%. Multi-gauge calibration markedly tightens parameter posterior distributions (more clustered kernel density) and enables robust parameter transfer to ungauged upstream sub-basins with only marginal KGE degradation (e.g., Hanzhong transfer KGE 0.516 vs. baseline 0.523 in validation). Under uniform parameterization, multi-gauge calibration forms convex arc-shaped Pareto fronts — a hard optimization ceiling — which spatially explicit parameterization breaks by expanding the feasible solution space.
- **Why it matters:** Justifies pursuing richer spatial parameterization and stronger multi-gauge constraints in parallel rather than sequentially; provides a template for Model–Data Infusion frameworks and ungauged basin prediction in nested catchments; open-source Python EVB framework released for VIC-5 deployment.
- **Caveats:** Single mesoscale basin in subtropical China; calibration limited to 40 trials due to compute constraints (authors note further optimization may refine results); MPR transfer functions rely heavily on SoilGrids data with inherent uncertainty; vegetation representation remains simplified; results may differ in arid or snow-dominated basins.
