# [Strategies for Predicting Flood Inundation in a Large and Complex Floodplain Based on Low‐Fidelity Hydrodynamic Models](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042481)

**Authors:** Wen Wang, Quan J. Wang, Rory Nathan

## TL;DR

LSG surrogate models applied to the Lower Brisbane River floodplain show that training on time-series data (LSG-TS) consistently outperforms training on maximum flood surfaces (LSG-Max).

## Summary

- **Problem:** High-fidelity hydrodynamic models are too slow for real-time flood forecasting or ensemble applications over large, complex floodplains with dam regulation, tidal effects, and interacting tributaries.
- **Method:** Applied the Low-Fidelity, Spatial Analysis and Gaussian Process Learning (LSG) surrogate model to the Lower Brisbane River floodplain; tested two variants — LSG-Max (trained directly on maximum flood surfaces) and LSG-TS (trained on full time-series, then deriving maxima); evaluated against historical and synthetic flood events.
- **Key result:** LSG-TS consistently outperformed LSG-Max across all test events; increasing low-fidelity model resolution significantly improved accuracy; initial naive implementation had limited accuracy until resolution was raised.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates feasibility of computationally efficient flood inundation surrogates for large floodplains with urban complexity, dam releases, and backwater effects — broadening operational applicability of the LSG framework.
- **Caveats:** Tested on one floodplain (Lower Brisbane); generalizability to other highly complex systems unverified; quantitative accuracy metrics not available from abstract; training data requirements and computational savings not specified.
