# [Precipitation Forecasting for Hydrologic Modeling in West-Central Florida using Seasonal Climate Outlooks](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2703-2026)

**Authors:** Manoj Shrestha, Hui Wang, Jeffrey S. Geurink et al.
**Venue:** HESS (Hydrology and Earth System Sciences) · **Date:** 2026-05-08 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-2703-2026
**Score:** 8/10 <sup>top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR
A hybrid ensemble sampling strategy—DTS in high-skill dry-season windows, PTS otherwise—translates NOAA 3-month outlooks into basin-scale rainfall forecasts with better skill than either method alone.

## Summary
- **Problem:** NOAA seasonal precipitation outlooks are issued at coarse spatial scales; their utility for local basin-scale hydrologic decision-making (water supply, wildfire) is untested, and converting probabilistic tercile categories into operational rainfall ensembles lacks a standardized approach.
- **Method:** Hindcast evaluation (1995–2019) of NOAA 3-month precipitation outlooks at 0.5-month lead over the Alafia and Hillsborough River Basins, Florida. Two non-parametric ensemble generators—Proportional Tercile Sampling (PTS) and Dominant Tercile Sampling (DTS)—sample pre-generated rainfall realizations conditioned on NOAA tercile forecasts. Skill assessed with categorical and probabilistic metrics against a Bayesian gauge-radar precipitation dataset.
- **Key result:** Forecast skill peaks in the dry season (October–February), especially for wet-tercile forecasts during El Niño years. DTS outperforms PTS in high-skill periods by leveraging dominant climate signals; PTS is more reliable in low-skill periods. A hybrid DTS (late fall/winter) + PTS (other seasons) strategy maximizes overall operational value.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a replicable, regionally adaptable framework for downscaling coarse NOAA outlooks to local basin scales, directly supporting operational water resources planning in climatically complex, ENSO-sensitive basins.
- **Caveats:** Study limited to two Florida basins with subtropical seasonality; skill during transitional seasons and non-ENSO years is lower; performance under climate non-stationarity (post-2019) not assessed.
