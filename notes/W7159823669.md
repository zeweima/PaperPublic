# [A Multi-Scale Geophysical–Geochemical Footprint of Persistent Methane Leakage at a Legacy Petroleum Well](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL122657)

**Authors:** Aaron G. Cahill, Benjamin Pullen, Cooper Pickering et al.

**Score:** <unclear from abstract>/10
**Source:** full text

## TL;DR

Persistent CH4 leakage from a decommissioned gas well imprints a nested, co-centered subsurface footprint: surface flux decays within ~0.12 m half-distance, EM conductivity within ~2 m, soil geochemistry broader still — all sharing a common source center.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Surface CH4 flux from legacy petroleum wells is meteorologically modulated and episodic, obscuring whether sustained leakage leaves a stable, spatially coherent subsurface imprint detectable beyond snapshot surveys.
- **Method:** Multi-year field campaign (2022, 2023, 2025) at a decommissioned hydraulically fractured gas well in NE British Columbia (TVD 1,345 m, buried ~3 m below surface). Integrated (1) repeated CH4–CO2 efflux mapping (dynamic chamber + laser analyzer), (2) electromagnetic induction surveys (Geonics EM38-MK4, 21×22 m grid, four coil spacings covering ~0.75–1.5 m depth), and (3) soil geochemical sampling (~40 cm depth) along transects from leakage center to background (~60 m). A composite Soil Leakage Index (SLI) was built from 8 variables (EC, S, Ca, Mg, Na, pH, Olsen P, CEC). Radial decay was fitted with offset exponentials; significance tested via permutation-bootstrap framework.
- **Key result:** Surface flux footprint centroid stable across years (<1.2 m offset for CO2, <0.4 m for CH4) despite large interannual magnitude variation. Flux' half-distance ~0.12 m (R² ≈ 0.98); EM conductivity anomaly (ΔCond) half-distance ~2.05 m (R² ≈ 0.94); SLI elevated throughout 0–4 m zone, returning to background at 60 m. All three domains share a statistically confirmed common center; ΔCond co-varies positively with leakage intensity.
- **Why it matters:** EM and soil geochemical signals integrate cumulative leakage history and are less sensitive to meteorological suppression than flux surveys — providing a stable, memory-bearing diagnostic for screening and prioritizing legacy well remediation and for monitoring CO2 storage sites where low-flux migration along legacy well pathways may evade flux-only detection.
- **Caveats:** Single-well study; geophysical and geochemical signals acquired in one campaign (April 2025) only. Well construction disturbance could generate anomalies without active leakage — anomaly presence alone is non-diagnostic. SLI spatial sampling too sparse for formal decay fitting. Footprint magnitudes will vary with soil texture, moisture, and geochemical buffering; multi-site validation needed.
