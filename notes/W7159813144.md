# [Ultra-narrowband perovskite single-crystal photodetector enabled by dynamic space charge region for portable concentration detection](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz9250)

**Authors:** Gangjian Hu, Hongyu Bian, Ding Luo et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz9250
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Perovskite single-crystal photodetectors using a dynamic space charge region achieve 9.0 nm FWHM narrowband detection and detect water pollutants at 10× lower LOD than Si detectors with optical filters.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Narrowband photodetectors for environmental monitoring (e.g., water pollutant detection) require complex optical filter combinations with traditional detectors; perovskite-based alternatives have lagged in practical narrowband performance.
- **Method:** Constructed a dynamic space charge region model in perovskite single crystals where mobile ions form a space charge region that suppresses above-bandgap photon response and amplifies band-edge photon response; wavelength selectivity is adjustable. Demonstrated portable application for water pollutant concentration detection.
- **Key result:** Achieved record FWHM of 9.0 nm for narrowband detection; limit of detection for water pollutants was an order of magnitude lower than commercial Si photodetectors paired with optical filters.
- **Why it matters:** Enables compact, filter-free, high-selectivity optical sensors for field-portable water quality monitoring — potentially simplifying environmental sensing instrumentation significantly.
- **Caveats:** Demonstrated on specific pollutant(s) in controlled settings; long-term stability of perovskite devices under real-world environmental conditions (humidity, temperature) is not addressed in the abstract; scalability and cost vs. existing systems unclear.
