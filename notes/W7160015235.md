## TL;DR

Earthworm activity accelerates soil phosphorus cycling via microbial pathways, traced using oxygen isotope ratios in phosphate (δ¹⁸O-PO₄).

## Summary

- **Problem:** How earthworms influence the turnover of plant-available phosphorus in soil, and whether the mechanism is direct (physical) or mediated through microbial activity.
- **Method:** δ¹⁸O in soil phosphate used as an isotopic tracer to distinguish enzymatic/microbial P cycling from abiotic release; earthworm manipulation experiment in soil mesocosms.
- **Key result:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Why it matters:** Clarifies the mechanistic role of soil fauna in P cycling; has implications for managing soil biological activity to improve P availability and reduce fertilizer inputs.
- **Caveats:** Abstract unavailable; scale (pot/field) and soil type specifics unclear; generalizability across earthworm species and soil P conditions not established.
