# [Deforestation-induced drying lowers Amazon climate threshold](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10456-0)

**Authors:** Nico Wunderling, Boris Sakschewski, Johan Rockström et al.
**Venue:** Nature · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10456-0
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Deforestation cuts the Amazon's critical warming threshold from ~3.8 °C to 1.5–1.9 °C, risking near system-wide collapse at 62–77% of forest area.

## Summary

- **Problem:** The interactive effect of global warming and land-use change on Amazon tipping risk is poorly quantified, particularly how deforestation lowers the warming threshold triggering large-scale forest collapse.
- **Method:** Dynamical systems model coupled to an atmospheric moisture-tracking model; assessed cascading transition risks under multiple Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs); spatial knock-on effects from drought propagation modelled across the full Amazon biome.
- **Key result:** Without deforestation, critical warming threshold is 3.7–4.0 °C with up to ~33% of Amazon forest losing stability. With 22–28% deforestation, threshold drops to 1.5–1.9 °C and 62–77% of the forest area faces near system-wide transition; the majority of transitions are driven by spatial drought cascades operating over hundreds to thousands of km.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that deforestation dramatically compounds climate risk — keeping warming below 1.5 °C is insufficient alone; simultaneous halt of deforestation and forest restoration are required to avoid high transition probabilities.
- **Caveats:** Dynamical systems model is necessarily simplified relative to full Earth system models; moisture-recycling parameterisation and SSP scenario uncertainty propagate into threshold estimates; ecological heterogeneity across the biome is likely underrepresented.
