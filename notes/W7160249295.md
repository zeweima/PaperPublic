# [Mid-Holocene retreat of the Greenland Ice Sheet indicated by subglacial methane release](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-01976-5)

**Authors:** J.E. Hatton, A. Stehrer-Polášková, P. A. Píka et al.
**Venue:** Nature Geoscience · **Date:** 2026-05-05 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-01976-5
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Radiocarbon-dated biogenic CH₄ (1.5–4.4 kyr BP) in western Greenland meltwater confirms the ice sheet was smaller during the Holocene Thermal Maximum and still emits ~715 t CH₄/yr from overridden organic matter.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Subglacial CH₄ sources, production pathways, and export magnitude beneath the Greenland Ice Sheet are poorly constrained, limiting carbon-cycle and ice-sheet model accuracy.
- **Method:** Extensive meltwater sampling across 26 streams along the entire western Greenland Ice Sheet margin; radiocarbon dating of dissolved CH₄; continuum degradation model applied to subglacial organic matter stocks.
- **Key result:** CH₄ in emerging subglacial meltwater is pervasive and biogenic with radiocarbon ages of 1.5–4.4 kyr BP, consistent with organic matter deposited during the Holocene Thermal Maximum (11–5 kyr BP) when the ice sheet was smaller, then overridden during glacial readvance. Current lateral CH₄ flux from land-terminating sectors is 715 (481–1,020) t/yr; degradation model projects continued release for ~200 more years.
- **Why it matters:** Quantifies a previously unaccounted glacial CH₄ source globally; isotopic evidence reveals a dynamic Holocene ice-sheet extent not captured in current ice-sheet models, with implications for paleoclimate reconstructions and carbon-cycle feedbacks under ongoing ice loss.
- **Caveats:** Flux estimate covers only land-terminating sectors of western Greenland; marine-terminating sectors and other margins not quantified; model assumes continuum degradation kinetics that may not capture spatial heterogeneity in organic matter quality.
