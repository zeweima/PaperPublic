# [Inflow Generation for Water System Planning in Multisite Studies Using Target Synthetic Streamflow Generation](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025WR042355)

## TL;DR

An optimization-based inverse framework applied to the Kirsch et al. (2013) parametric streamflow generator achieves exact targeting of monthly mean and standard deviation at 14 multisite locations in 3.22 hr per 100 scenarios — 50× faster than non-parametric multisite alternatives.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Bottom-up stress testing of multisite water systems requires synthetic streamflow scenarios that hit specific magnitude, variability, and seasonality targets simultaneously; existing stochastic generators cannot guarantee this, and optimization-based alternatives are either single-site or computationally prohibitive (4–169 hr per 100 scenarios for prior frameworks).
- **Method:** Inverse (optimization) approach applied to the Kirsch et al. (2013) parametric, log-normal, Cholesky-decomposition streamflow generator. Differential evolution (SciPy) minimizes |resultant monthly mean change − target| + |resultant SD change − target| separately per month and location; feasible/infeasible regions of the exposure space are explicitly mapped and partially infeasible targets are substituted with nearest feasible alternatives. Monthly sequences are disaggregated to daily via k-nearest neighbors (Nowak et al. 2010). Tested on the Winnipeg River Basin (136,840 km², 14 inflow locations, 1980–2017 observed record) with a 19-member CMIP5 GCM ensemble (BaySys project) providing future seasonality targets.
- **Key result:** Optimization reduced mean and SD deviations from target to exactly 0% across all 14 locations and months (vs. up to +123% mean / +438% SD deviation for non-optimized scenarios at the highest-CV location). Autocorrelation RMSE fell from 0.09 (non-optimized) to 0.08 (optimized) at both representative locations; cross-correlation RMSE fell from 0.12 to 0.05 at the high-CV location. Wilcoxon rank-sum and Levene's tests show optimized scenarios preserve historical distribution median and variance (p > 0.05 for all months). Runtime: 3.22 hr per 100 scenarios at 14 locations on a laptop-class CPU, versus 169 hr for a comparable 18-location non-parametric framework. Stress-testing application shows annual hydropower reliability at Winnipeg River requires ≥ +6% mean inflow increase for system-wide success, and Shellmouth Dam flood frequency becomes sensitive to variability increases above ~20%.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a transparent, computationally tractable alternative to GCM-driven (top-down) and non-parametric bottom-up approaches; directly controls the three operational drivers of water system behavior (magnitude, variability, seasonality) at multiple sites without a hydrological model; Python code and processed data publicly archived (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.17195525). Complements top-down GCM projections by uniformly covering uncertainty space, including conditions outside the GCM envelope.
- **Caveats:** Log-normal retransformation imposes a mathematically infeasible region in exposure space (non-parametric generators do not have this constraint). Autocorrelation and cross-correlation are preserved implicitly, not explicitly optimized — underestimation of short-lag autocorrelation persists (RMSE 0.08). Daily disaggregation via k-NN resamples historical daily patterns, which may be unrealistic under large seasonality shifts. Framework does not ensure physical plausibility of climate forcing.
