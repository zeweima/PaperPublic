# [Development and Evaluation of a Simple Geometric Radiation Absorption Model for Discontinuous Canopies With Ellipsoidal Crown Shapes](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009386)

**Authors:** María A. Ponce de León, Kyle Knipper, Nicolás Bambach et al.
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009386
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

A geometric binomial model with ellipsoidal crown geometry outperforms Beer's law and clumping-factor models for radiation absorption in row-oriented discontinuous canopies.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Large-scale canopy models apply Beer's law or simple clumping corrections that overpredict radiation absorption in heterogeneous, row-oriented canopies (e.g., orchards) because they assume canopy homogeneity.
- **Method:** Developed a geometric binomial radiation absorption model that accounts for scattering, variable radiative path lengths through ellipsoidal crowns, diffuse radiation, and crown shadow overlap. Evaluated against a 3D leaf-resolving model (Helios) using virtually generated canopies and field measurements in almond and olive orchards.
- **Key result:** Beer's law considerably overpredicted radiation absorption across the tested canopies. The binomial model achieved index of agreement 0.91–0.99 across all canopies; the variable-clumping-factor model showed errors increasing with radiative path length.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a computationally simple replacement for Beer's law in large-scale canopy energy balance and ET models, directly improving estimation of photosynthesis and transpiration in orchard and savanna-type landscapes where row structure is dominant.
- **Caveats:** Requires additional canopy geometry inputs (crown dimensions, row orientation); tested only in almond and olive orchards — generality to other discontinuous canopy types (e.g., tropical woodlands) is undemonstrated.
