# [Phytoplankton and Temperature Control Seasonal Dynamics of Greenhouse Gases in a Large River](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009300)

**Authors:** Matthias Koschorreck, Claudia Schütze, Uta Ködel et al.
**Venue:** JGR Biogeosciences · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jg009300
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Five years of Elbe River GHG monitoring show phytoplankton photosynthesis drives summer CO₂ drawdown while temperature drives CH₄ peaks, with ML models able to predict all three gases from seasonal proxies alone.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Temporal variability and co-controls on river CO₂:CH₄:N₂O concentration ratios and fluxes are poorly constrained, limiting understanding of rivers as GHG sources.
- **Method:** Continuous 5-year monitoring of CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O at two sites on the German River Elbe; correlation analysis with water quality, light, and chlorophyll data; data-driven ML models to identify predictors of GHG concentrations.
- **Key result:** CO₂ ranged 2.8–125 μmol L⁻¹ (below saturation in summer, driven by planktonic photosynthesis); CH₄ ranged 0.0006–0.85 μmol L⁻¹ (summer peak correlated with temperature and POC); N₂O (0.007–0.07 μmol L⁻¹) near saturation year-round, controlled by temperature-dependent solubility. Summer CH₄ emission increases were compensated by CO₂ uptake. ML models predicted all three GHG concentrations from seasonal indicators without water quality parameters.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes phytoplankton metabolism as the dominant seasonal CO₂ control in a large turbid river and demonstrates that simple seasonal proxies may suffice for GHG prediction, reducing monitoring burden for river carbon budgets.
- **Caveats:** Single river (Elbe); only two sites limits spatial inference; CH₄ dynamics were more locally controlled (hydrodynamics, sedimentation) and may not generalize; ML models validated internally but not across independent river systems.
