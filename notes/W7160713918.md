## TL;DR

Long-term straw mulching combined with N fertilization jointly optimizes wheat yield, soil quality, and N₂O emissions in dryland cropping systems.

## Summary
- **Problem:** Dryland cropping systems face trade-offs between maximizing wheat yield and soil health versus minimizing N₂O greenhouse gas emissions; the integrated long-term effects of straw mulching and N rate on all three outcomes are unclear.
- **Method:** Long-term field experiment (duration unclear from abstract) in a dryland cropping system testing combinations of straw mulching and nitrogen fertilization rates on winter wheat; measured grain yield, soil quality indicators, and N₂O fluxes.
- **Key result:** <unclear from abstract>
- **Why it matters:** Identifies management combinations that can co-optimize productivity, soil carbon/quality, and climate mitigation in rainfed wheat systems — directly relevant to sustainable dryland agriculture under N-use-efficiency and GHG-reduction goals.
- **Caveats:** Abstract unavailable; duration of long-term experiment, specific N rates tested, and magnitude of yield and N₂O responses cannot be assessed. Findings may be site-specific to the study region in China.
