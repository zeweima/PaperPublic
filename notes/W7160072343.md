# [Record-breaking glacier mass loss in Central Asia in 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6712)

**Authors:** Lander Van Tricht, Marin Kneib, Matthias Huss et al.
**Venue:** Environmental Research Letters · **Date:** 2026-04-30 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6712
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Central Asian glaciers lost ~30 ± 6 km³ (~2% of total mass) in 2025, the most negative mass-balance year on record, driven by anomalous spring–summer warmth and early melt onset.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Whether 2025 constituted a regional mass-balance record for Central Asian glaciers and what climate forcings drove it.
- **Method:** In situ observations combined with regional simulations using the Global Glacier Evolution Model (GloGEM) across ~4,000 glaciers >1 km² in Tien Shan and Pamir; climate attribution via reanalysis.
- **Key result:** 64% of glaciers >1 km² set their most negative annual mass balance on record; total regional loss ~30 ± 6 km³ (26 ± 9 Gt), ~2% of present-day total mass. Loss was strongest in western Tien Shan and western Pamir (−2 to −4%) and more moderate in eastern sectors (−1 to −1.5%).
- **Why it matters:** Loss magnitude exceeds prior records across a spatially coherent region; early snow loss accelerated ice-albedo feedbacks, suggesting this level of loss could become the new normal under continued warming.
- **Caveats:** Uncertainty bounds on volume loss are substantial (±6 km³); not all individual glaciers set records; GloGEM performance at tails of the mass-balance distribution needs validation.
