# [Integrated climate effects on nitrogen cycles in global grasslands](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec5940)

**Authors:** Miao Zheng, Cui J, Luxi Cheng et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-05-06 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec5940
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Climate warming drives +19.8% N harvest and +28.2% N surplus in global grasslands 1980–2020, with a projected USD $69 billion additional cost by 2050.

## Summary

- **Problem:** How do multiple simultaneous climate change factors (warming, precipitation variability, elevated CO₂) jointly alter nitrogen cycling in global grasslands, and what are the economic consequences?
- **Method:** Global nitrogen budget analysis covering 150 countries, 1980–2020; compared scenarios with and without climate change; projected to 2050 under SSP2-RCP4.5.
- **Key result:** Climate warming increased nitrogen harvest by 19.8%, biological N fixation by 8.8%, and N surplus by 28.2%; precipitation variability amplified these increases with spatial heterogeneity; elevated CO₂ partially offset surplus by enhancing plant N uptake. Under SSP2-RCP4.5 by 2050, N input, harvest, and surplus projected to increase by 22.3, 7.2, and 15.1 million tonnes yr⁻¹ respectively vs. baseline; climate-induced N budget changes could cost up to USD $69 billion from health and ecosystem impacts.
- **Why it matters:** Quantifies multi-factor climate forcing on grassland N cycles globally, showing warming dominates over CO₂ fertilization effects; provides economic framing for reactive nitrogen losses that can inform grassland management policy.
- **Caveats:** Abstract-only detail; N budget model uncertainties across 150 countries likely large; SSP2-RCP4.5 projections depend on emission scenario assumptions; spatial heterogeneity of precipitation effects not fully characterized.
