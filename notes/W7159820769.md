# [Shrub Encroachment Constrains Precipitation‐Driven Shifts in Herbaceous Biomass‐Diversity Relationships](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70890)

**Authors:** Hui Li, Xiaoan Zuo, Fons van der Plas et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70890
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Shrub encroachment dampens precipitation-driven shifts in the herbaceous biomass–richness unimodal relationship by decoupling diversity from soil water availability.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Grassland biomass–diversity relationships follow a unimodal pattern that shifts with precipitation, but whether shrub encroachment alters this precipitation sensitivity is unknown.
- **Method:** Meta-synthesis of globally distributed precipitation manipulation experiments comparing grasslands vs. shrublands; supported by a 5-year field experiment in a desert steppe in Northern China.
- **Key result:** In grasslands, reduced precipitation shifts the unimodal biomass–richness peak toward lower biomass and increased precipitation shifts it toward higher biomass; in shrub-dominated systems, the peak shows no significant shift across precipitation extremes. Precipitation affects richness mainly through soil water availability in grasslands but through the fast–slow leaf economics spectrum in shrublands.
- **Why it matters:** Woody encroachment into grasslands reduces ecosystem responsiveness to precipitation variability, with implications for predicting grassland biodiversity and productivity under altered rainfall regimes.
- **Caveats:** Shrubland experiment is limited to a single desert steppe region in Northern China; the global synthesis relies on heterogeneous manipulation experiments that may differ in duration, intensity, and shrub cover metrics.
