# [Volcanic forcing of global climate cooling at the Younger Dryas onset preserved in North American sediments](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec9030)

**Authors:** Lucien Nana Yobo, A. D. Brandon, Sydney O'Brien et al.
**Venue:** Science Advances · **Date:** 2026-04-29 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec9030
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Multiproxy North American sediment records link a cluster of bipolar volcanic eruptions (~12.98–12.87 ka) to Younger Dryas onset, offering a volcanically driven alternative to impact hypotheses.

## Summary

- **Problem:** The trigger of the Younger Dryas (YD) cooling event remains contested; impact-based hypotheses dominate but lack regionally consistent geochemical support.
- **Method:** Os isotope ratios measured across multiple North American depositional environments, correlated against bipolar volcanic eruption records from Greenland and Antarctic ice cores spanning ~12.98–12.87 thousand years ago.
- **Key result:** Os ratios align across depositional settings and coincide with a volcanic cluster whose cumulative radiative forcing exceeds the most volcanically active intervals of the Common Era; hemispheric asymmetry of eruptions implies forcing sufficient to disrupt AMOC and drive rapid Northern Hemisphere cooling.
- **Why it matters:** Provides multiproxy, regionally consistent evidence that volcanism — not an extraterrestrial impact — initiated YD cooling, reframing interpretation of one of the most studied abrupt climate events.
- **Caveats:** Abstract does not quantify radiative forcing magnitude precisely; causal pathway from volcanism to AMOC disruption is inferred rather than directly modeled; single isotope system (Os) anchors the geochemical argument.
