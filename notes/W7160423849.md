# [Demographic and Structural Variability Modulate Growth Dynamics in European Beech Primary Forests](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70902)

**Authors:** Krešimír Begović, Jakob Pavlin, Thomas Langbehn et al.
**Venue:** Global Change Biology · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70902
**Score:** 8/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Demographic and structural heterogeneity buffer beech forest productivity under warming, but this buffering collapses under severe drought stress.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Network-wide beech growth models ignore intraspecific demographic variability, potentially underestimating both adaptive capacity and productivity losses under climate warming.
- **Method:** 530 plots, ~11,000 trees from primary Fagus sylvatica forests across a broad environmental gradient in central and southeastern Europe; dendrochronological analysis of radial growth sensitivity to climate; demographic stratification by tree size and age.
- **Key result:** Large trees showed stronger sensitivity to prior-summer heat stress; small trees tracked spring/summer moisture. Productivity losses outweighed gains across the full network under warming. Growth declines concentrated in mid-sized and large trees; small trees gained in mesic regions, old trees gained in xeric regions. Severe summer droughts triggered multi-year growth legacies only in xeric regions.
- **Why it matters:** Standard growth projections that ignore demographic structure will misestimate beech forest carbon sink trajectories; structural complexity does buffer productivity but fails under extreme stress, which has management implications for European forest conservation.
- **Caveats:** Limited to primary (unmanaged) forests; transferability to managed stands unclear. Dominant climatic predictors (prior-summer Tmax, climatic water balance) vary by region, complicating pan-European generalization.
