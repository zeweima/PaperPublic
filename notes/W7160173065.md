# [In‐Lake GHG Production Constrained by Lake DOM Mass Balance in a Boreal Catchment](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041297)

**Authors:** Xiang Lu, Francois Clayer, Peter Dörsch et al.
**Venue:** Water Resources Research · **Date:** 2026-05-01 · **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041297
**Score:** 9/10<sup> top pick</sup>
**Source:** abstract

## TL;DR

Wetter future climate will suppress boreal lake GHG production more than warming amplifies it by shortening water residence time.

## Summary

- **Problem:** Boreal humic lakes are known GHG hotspots, but concurrent organic C and inorganic C mass balances constraining in-lake mineralization rates are rarely constructed, leaving future climate controls on lake GHG production uncertain.
- **Method:** Dense monitoring of DOC, dissolved CO₂, and CH₄ at inlets and outlet of headwater lake Langtjern (Norway) throughout 2023 (wet year); constructed full organic and inorganic C budgets; correlated mineralization and CH₄ production with water temperature and hydrology.
- **Key result:** DOC inputs (14.7 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹) exceeded export (12.4 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹); lake evaded 1.50 g C m⁻² (catchment area) yr⁻¹ as CO₂ and 0.012 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ as CH₄. Mineralization rates from organic (1.30 ± 0.43 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹) and inorganic (1.80 ± 0.83 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹) budgets agreed well. Catchment C inputs were precipitation-driven; DOC removal was limited by water residence time.
- **Why it matters:** Precipitation increase (wetter climate) reduces aquatic processing time and thus in-lake GHG production, potentially outweighing the temperature-driven enhancement—a counterintuitive result for boreal carbon budget projections.
- **Caveats:** Single wet year (2023) may not represent interannual variability; main budget uncertainties stem from catchment input estimates and lake evasion measurements; single lake limits generalizability across catchment types.
