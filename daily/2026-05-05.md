### Climate & global change

**Dust storms: Hidden drivers of extreme rainfall and global precipitation shifts**
Liu et al. · Science Advances · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw6864](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw6864) · [notes](../notes/W7158590478.md)

Global observations of post-dust-storm accumulated precipitation (7-day window) combined with numerical simulations show that dust storms boost precipitation by up to 9.6 mm relative to dust-free baselines. In high-anthropogenic-aerosol regions, dust suppresses light rainfall and amplifies heavy events; dust particle ice-nucleation is the mechanistic driver, distinct from the effect of anthropogenic aerosols alone. Dust aerosol loading must be accounted for in regional heavy-precipitation forecasting and attribution, especially where anthropogenic aerosols are rising.

**Growing impacts of fire smoke on ozone pollution and associated mortality burden in the United States**
Li et al. · Science Advances · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec2903](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec2903) · [notes](../notes/W7159095976.md)

Fire smoke's share of US ozone-pollution-linked mortality reached 61.5% in 2023, with a growing trend over the study period. Standard air-quality regulations focused on anthropogenic NOx/VOCs may increasingly miss fire-driven ozone mortality as wildfire frequency and extent expand under climate change.

**The pace of meeting carbon emission targets alters regional climate risks**
Park, Yeh, King · Science Advances · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec4566](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec4566) · [notes](../notes/W7158537509.md)

Multi-model comparison across 29 CMIP models shows that faster emission trajectories produce greater land warming and higher heat-wave exposure in 80% and 85% of land regions, respectively, relative to slower pathways reaching the identical cumulative carbon budget — driven by reduced deep-ocean heat uptake. Temporal pacing of emissions is an independent determinant of regional climate risk beyond the carbon budget framing that currently dominates mitigation policy.

**Volcanic forcing of global climate cooling at the Younger Dryas onset preserved in North American sediments**
Nana Yobo et al. · Science Advances · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec9030](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec9030) · [notes](../notes/W7158767479.md)

Os isotope ratios measured across multiple North American depositional environments align consistently with a bipolar volcanic eruption cluster spanning ~12.98–12.87 ka, whose cumulative radiative forcing exceeds the most volcanically active Common Era intervals. Hemispheric eruption asymmetry implies forcing sufficient to disrupt AMOC and drive rapid Northern Hemisphere cooling. Provides multiproxy, regionally consistent evidence for volcanism — not extraterrestrial impact — as the trigger of the Younger Dryas.

### Machine learning / methods

**Physics-based models outperform AI weather forecasts of record-breaking extremes**
Zhang, Fischer, Zscheischler et al. · Science Advances · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec1433](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec1433) · [notes](../notes/W7158534923.md)

ECMWF HRES consistently outperforms GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, and Fuxi at forecasting record-breaking heat, cold, and wind events across nearly all lead times — AI models underestimate frequency and intensity of record exceedances, underpredicting hot records and overpredicting cold records, with errors growing with exceedance magnitude. A systematic extrapolation failure precisely where accurate forecasts matter most, with implications for using AI weather models in early-warning and disaster-management applications under a warming climate.

**Physics-Informed Neural Learning for State Reconstruction and Parameter Identification in Coupled Greenhouse Climate Dynamics**
Biswas, Ansari, Akhtar · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-05-04
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02524) · [notes](../notes/arxiv-2605.02524.md)

Coupled PINN framework embedding a reduced-order physically motivated model jointly reconstructs indoor temperature and humidity while recovering governing physical parameters from sparse, noisy sensor data, outperforming purely data-driven baselines — most markedly for latent humidity dynamics. Demonstrates that physics-informed learning delivers interpretable, parameter-aware state reconstruction in data-scarce enclosed agricultural environments; approach is extensible to other environmental monitoring systems with limited sensors.

**Smart Ensemble Learning Framework for Predicting Groundwater Heavy Metal Pollution**
Ansah-Narh et al. · arXiv (stat.ML) · 2026-04-29
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00056) · [notes](../notes/arxiv-2605.00056.md)

Gaussian copula transformation with a stacked Lasso ensemble achieves R²=0.96, RMSE=0.19 for groundwater Heavy Metal Pollution Index in Ghana's Densu Basin — raw-scale models produce over-optimistic fits (R²≈1.0), while log transformation improves stability (SVM R²=0.93). DBSCAN clustering identifies Fe and Mn as primary HPI drivers. Distribution-aware preprocessing is critical before applying ML to skewed geochemical indices; copula-based ensembles yield spatially credible contamination maps.
