**Sources of Uncertainty in Ocean Net Primary Productivity Projections Under Climate Change**
Le Grix, Tagliabue · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl119652](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl119652) · [notes](../notes/W7159778153.md)

Inter-model variance decomposition across 7 CMIP6 ESMs shows absolute NPP uncertainty is dominated by the preindustrial mean state (59%) and preindustrial seasonal cycle (26%) throughout 1850–2100, while NPPA uncertainty roughly doubles by 2100 under SSP5-8.5 (±1.3 → ±5.6 μgC m⁻² y⁻¹), driven by shifted decadal mean (38%) and shifted seasonal cycle (33%). Signal-to-noise ratio for NPPA reaches only 0.31 globally even under SSP5-8.5, with S/N > 1 over <1% of ocean area — versus 93% for SST anomaly. Constraining historical NPP mean and seasonal cycle in ESMs is the highest-leverage action for reducing projection uncertainty; the Arctic shifted seasonal cycle emerges as a newly identified major driver under sea-ice retreat.

**Attributing Upper-Tropospheric Warm Biases in CMIP6 Models to Ice Cloud-Radiation Interaction Deficiencies Over Tropical Oceans**
Li et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl120130](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl120130) · [notes](../notes/W7159826985.md)

26 CMIP6 models grouped by falling-ice radiative treatment show that models with separate prognostic falling ice (SON2/MG2 scheme) reduce upper-tropospheric warm biases by 26–51% (absolute bias 1.21 K vs. 1.52–1.83 K at 200 hPa) relative to models omitting or lumping falling-ice effects. The mechanism runs through SST — omitting falling-ice radiative effects weakens trade-wind stress, warms SSTs El Niño-like, and drives condensational heating that propagates to the upper troposphere. SON2-type models are underrepresented in CMIP6, so ensemble means understate falling-ice importance; adopting MG2-class schemes in CMIP7 is a concrete path to reducing this persistent bias.

### Hydrology & water resources

**Quantifying the Rapid Propagation of Rainfall and Evapotranspiration Signals Into Soils**
Gao et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121730](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121730) · [notes](../notes/W7159786113.md)

ERRA (Ensemble Rainfall-Runoff Analysis), a nonparametric deconvolution applied to hourly soil moisture at 4 Swiss sites (2 grasslands, 2 forests, 5–18 yr), shows that ET produces roughly equal-magnitude but opposite-sign soil water recharge responses to precipitation at all depths. Precipitation response peaks within 1 hr and ceases within 1–2 hr; ET response persists ~3 hr at grasslands and 5–6 hr at forests due to root vascular lags. Peak P-driven recharge is damped 81–96% by the deepest sensor (30–60 cm) with negligible lag; net recharge fractions reaching depth are ~5% (grassland) and ~15% (forest). These empirically de-mixed impulse response functions provide direct constraints for testing LSMs and soil-plant-atmosphere continuum models.

**Inflow Generation for Water System Planning in Multisite Studies Using Target Synthetic Streamflow Generation**
Gozini et al. · Water Resources Research · 2026-05
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042355](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042355) · [notes](../notes/W7159600021.md)

Optimization-based inverse framework applied to the Kirsch et al. (2013) parametric streamflow generator achieves exact targeting of monthly mean and standard deviation at 14 multisite locations in the Winnipeg River Basin (136,840 km²), reducing mean/SD deviations from target to exactly 0% vs. up to +123%/+438% for non-optimized scenarios at the highest-CV location. Runtime is 3.22 hr per 100 scenarios on laptop-class CPU, versus 169 hr for a comparable non-parametric framework. Cross-correlation RMSE fell from 0.12 to 0.05 at the high-CV location. Stress-testing shows annual hydropower reliability requires ≥+6% mean inflow increase for system-wide success; Shellmouth Dam flood frequency becomes sensitive to variability increases above ~20%.

**From Hydraulic Heads to Dollars and Decision: It's Time to Integrate Groundwater in Coastal Risk Assessment**
Gonzalez-Duque et al. · Water Resources Research · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041035](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr041035) · [notes](../notes/W7159683209.md)

Commentary proposing a hazard–exposure–vulnerability framework for monetizing coastal groundwater hazards (groundwater shoaling, salinization, subsurface flooding) analogous to flood risk accounting. Illustrative numbers from prior work: pavement modifications can eliminate 80–90% of service-life reduction under 0.3 m sea-level rise; salinity >3,500 ppm accelerates pipe corrosion. Four knowledge gaps identified: lack of in-situ infrastructure corrosion monitoring, unknown LID–groundwater feedbacks, uncommunicated hazard uncertainty, and limited translational science. Calls for a Coastal Groundwater Observatory (~2,000 gauges, analogous to GLOSS tide gauge network) as a near-term step.

**Asynchronous Landslide Seasonality Across the United States**
Luna et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121707](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl121707) · [notes](../notes/W7159807257.md)

Von Mises circular distributions fit via Bayesian MCMC to 56,257 landslides across 67 NWS County Warning Areas reveal strongly asynchronous regional seasonality: concentrated winter on the West Coast, spring in parts of central/eastern US, summer in the Rockies, autumn in Puerto Rico, and year-round in southeastern Alaska and coastal Mid-Atlantic. Slide vs. fall seasonality is credibly distinguishable in only 2 of 35 CWAs tested (Juneau AK; Salt Lake City UT). In western Washington, a credible trend of 10 days/decade (95% HDI: 1.4–20) toward a later season was detected 1990–2020, shifting mean season ~31 days from early January to early February.

### Biogeochemistry & soils

**A Multi-Scale Geophysical–Geochemical Footprint of Persistent Methane Leakage at a Legacy Petroleum Well**
Cahill et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122657](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122657) · [notes](../notes/W7159823669.md)

Multi-year field campaign (2022–2025) at a decommissioned hydraulically fractured gas well in NE British Columbia integrates CH₄–CO₂ efflux mapping, EM induction surveys, and soil geochemical sampling. Surface flux footprint centroid is stable across years (<1.2 m offset) despite large interannual magnitude variation; flux half-distance ~0.12 m (R² ≈ 0.98). EM conductivity anomaly half-distance ~2.05 m (R² ≈ 0.94); a composite Soil Leakage Index (SLI) across 8 variables remains elevated throughout 0–4 m zone. All three domains share a statistically confirmed common center, offering a stable memory-bearing diagnostic less sensitive to meteorological suppression than flux surveys alone — relevant for legacy well remediation prioritization and CO₂ storage monitoring.

### Agroecosystem process & modeling

**A comparative study of methods to quantify wheat yield penalty attributable to soil sodicity across north-eastern Australia**
Lai et al. · Soil & Tillage Research · 2026-04-29
[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107209](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.still.2026.107209) · [notes](../notes/W7159774072.md)

Three approaches to quantify sodicity-induced wheat yield penalties across Australia's Northern Grains Region (65–88% of wheat land sodic) produce systematically divergent regional mean estimates: Empirical 1 (iNDVI proxy, kriged) = 0.213 Mg ha⁻¹, Empirical 2 (yield monitor data, 17 farms) = 0.078 Mg ha⁻¹, APSIM = 0.259 Mg ha⁻¹ (2000–2016). APSIM penalties peak under wet conditions (0.328 Mg ha⁻¹) because higher yield potential amplifies absolute deficits; Empirical 2 peaks under dry conditions (0.198 Mg ha⁻¹) due to hydraulic limitation intensification. The three approaches are complementary across scales — Empirical 1 for stable regional attribution, Empirical 2 for within-field diagnosis, APSIM for counterfactual scenario analysis.

### Machine learning / methods

Sun et al. · Water Resources Research · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042780](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025wr042780) · [notes](../notes/W7159683847.md)

TabPFN (Transformer pre-trained on synthetic structural causal models for in-context few-shot learning) maps groundwater HCO₃⁻ across China at 0.1° from 1,350 samples and 12 selected predictors, achieving test-set R²=0.830, RMSE=30.14 mg/L vs. R²=0.384–0.777 for RF, XGBoost, LightGBM, SVR and others; advantage is most pronounced at 10–50% training-data fractions with no hyperparameter tuning. **M-CaStLe: Uncovering Local Causal Structures in Multivariate Space-Time Gridded Data**
Nichol et al. · arXiv · 2026-05-01
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00398) · [notes](../notes/arxiv-2605.00398.md)

M-CaStLe extends the CaStLe meta-algorithm to V variables per grid cell, jointly discovering within- and cross-variable causal structure by pooling 3×3 Moore neighborhoods into shared tensors — reducing search complexity from O(2^{N²V}) to O(2^{9V}) and converting T=7 samples per location into L_eff ≈ 5,384 effective samples (~769×). On VAR benchmarks (V=1–6, 4×4 grids, 31,140 simulations), F1 ≈ 0.96 at V=1; on a Mt. Pinatubo E3SMv2 case with 4 aerosol/radiative variables, F1=0.95 vs. F1=0.00 for PCMCI on spatially aggregated data. ADR PDE tests yield median advection-angle error 4.76° and reaction-graph F1 = 1.0 (median). Applicable to co-located Earth system variables (temperature, soil moisture, aerosol species) in high-dimensional gridded systems where standard causal discovery fails.

### Remote sensing & observations

**Prevalent Greening Conceals the Forgone Ecological Potential of Forest Loss in Southeast Asia**
Zhao et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026
[https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121593](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl121593) · [notes](../notes/W7159856158.md)

Cloud-robust 30-m Landsat–MODIS fusion covering 2001–2022 shows regional net EVI increase of 5.47% and NIRv increase of 12.47% across Southeast Asia, yet propensity-score matching (1-to-3, matched on topography, climate, pre-disturbance trends) reveals forest conversion created lasting deficits of ~16.12% in greenness and ~6.06% in productivity relative to stable-forest counterfactuals. Forest-to-cropland transitions caused the largest drops (EVI: -8.68 to -10.40%; NIRv: -12.48 to -14.50%); Cambodia was the largest hotspot (EVI deficit 72.10%). Post-2015 deceleration in forest loss recovered 1.49% greenness potential. Surface greening metrics used to infer ecosystem health can be deeply misleading where land-use change co-occurs with environmental change.

**Efficient Spatio-Temporal Vegetation Pixel Classification with Vision Transformers**

Standard ViT (6 layers, D=256) adapted for 1D spatio-temporal tokens on UAV and phenocam image time series in Brazilian Cerrado matches or beats a multi-temporal CNN while cutting FLOPs 14×. **Foundation AI Models for Aerosol Optical Depth Estimation from PACE Satellite Data**
Tushar, Purushotham · IGARSS 2026 · 2026-05-01
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00678) · [notes](../notes/arxiv-2605.00678.md)

ViTCG — compact Vision Transformer (8.82 M parameters) with channel-wise spectral grouping applied to 96×96-pixel patches of PACE OCI L1B radiance (291 bands) — retrieves AOD at 550 nm with MSE 0.0141, IOA 0.8552, validated against 125 AERONET Level-2 sites. **Remote SAMsing: From Segment Anything to Segment Everything**
Carvalho et al. · arXiv · 2026-04-30
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00256) · [notes](../notes/arxiv-2605.00256.md)

Multi-pass black-masking pipeline wraps SAM2 — painting accepted-mask pixels black after each pass to simplify the scene for the next pass, with IoU/stability thresholds decaying only when per-pass coverage gain stagnates — raising segmentation coverage from 30–68% (single-pass) to 91–98% across seven large RS scenes (5 cm–4.78 m GSD). A parameter-free Union-Find best-match merge reconstructs objects fragmented across tile boundaries. Per-class Det@0.5: buildings 95%, cars 82–93%, pivot irrigation 100%. A 1.94-billion-pixel Potsdam mosaic achieved 97% coverage and 81.8% ASA across 40,618 objects in ~20 h on one GPU. Tile size functions as an interpretable scale parameter (~50 m per tile is a practical guideline).
